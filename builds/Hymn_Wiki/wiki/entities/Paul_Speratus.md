# Paul Speratus

## The Poet of the First Hymn-Book

Paul Speratus was one of [[Martin_Luther]]'s most gifted co-laborers in the work of evangelical hymnody. He was among the very first contributors to the "Achtliederbuch" (1524), the first Protestant hymnal, contributing three of its eight hymns --- the most after Luther himself.

## Life and Service

Speratus wrote his most famous hymn, "To Us Salvation Now Is Come" (*Es ist das Heil uns kommen her*), while imprisoned in a Moravian jail, having been cast there for his bold embrace of Lutheran teachings. The experience of writing that great confessional hymn from a prison cell is itself an emblem of the Reformation spirit.

Upon his release he traveled immediately to Wittenberg and joined Luther and the Reformers. He later became the leader of the Reformation movement in Prussia and was chosen Bishop of Pomerania before his death in 1551.

## The Great Confessional Hymn

"To Us Salvation Now Is Come" has been called "the poetic counterpart of Luther's preface to the Epistle to the Romans" --- a hymn that sets the doctrine of grace alone, faith alone, in verse that ordinary people could sing. Luther himself is said to have wept tears of joy when he heard a street singer outside his Wittenberg window singing it.

The hymn concludes with a beautiful paraphrase of the Lord's Prayer:

> *All blessing, honor, thanks, and praise*
> *To Father, Son, and Spirit,*
> *The God who saved us by His grace,*
> *All glory to His merit...*
> *Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done*
> *In earth, as 'tis in heaven.*

Speratus exemplifies the early Reformation hymn-writer: a man whose faith cost him freedom, who responded with song, and who gave the Church a treasure that endured.
