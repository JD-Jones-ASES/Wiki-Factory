# Mary Artemisia Lathbury

Mary Artemisia Lathbury (1841–1913) was an American hymn writer, artist, and editor whose songs were composed especially for use in the religious exercises at Chautauqua.

## Life and Background

Lathbury was the daughter of Rev. John Lathbury, a Methodist minister, and was born in Manchester, New York, on August 10, 1841. Two of her brothers, Albert Augustus and Clarence Lathbury, also served as ministers of the Methodist Episcopal Church. After leaving school she became an art teacher, and later an artist and illustrator, and served as the literary and illustrations editor of a picture-lesson paper. Her address in later life was East Orange, New Jersey.

She was the author of some eight or ten small volumes, but she is most widely and favorably known through her songs and hymns, which were composed especially for use in the religious exercises at Chautauqua.

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

According to Nutter and Tillett, among the new hymns added by the compilers to the Methodist Hymnal, none were more universally admired than the two short gems by Miss Lathbury. In both of her hymns the meters are beautifully suited to the sentiment of the hymns. They expressed a rare combination of aesthetic gift and devotional atmosphere in beautiful lyrics.

The hymns represented in the collection include "Break thou the bread of life" and "Day is dying in the west."

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett give a warm portrait of Lathbury as a figure whose contributions came through the distinctive Chautauqua context — a summer assembly for adult education and religious enrichment in western New York. Her hymns were written for that setting and proved durable enough for inclusion in the Methodist Hymnal itself, where they were counted among the finest new additions.
