# John Zundel

John Zundel was a German-born organist who made his mark in American sacred music through his long association with Plymouth Church in Brooklyn, where he served under the celebrated Henry Ward Beecher --- and through one hymn tune above all others.

## Life and Career

Zundel was born in 1815 and came to America, settling in New York. He served as organist at Plymouth Congregational Church in Brooklyn, Henry Ward Beecher's famous pulpit, for many years. This position placed him at the center of American Protestant life at its most influential point.

He compiled *Christian Heart Songs* (1870), a collection that brought together many of the hymns used at Plymouth Church. He was an accomplished organist and a competent composer. He died in 1882.

## "Beecher" / "Love Divine"

The tune that has given Zundel his lasting place in hymnody is known variously as "Beecher" (after his famous employer) or "Love Divine" (after the hymn it accompanies). It is the melody most commonly sung with Charles Wesley's great hymn "Love Divine, All Loves Excelling" in American hymnals. The tune has the warm, forward-moving quality that Wesley's ecstatic text demands, and the two have proven inseparable for generations of American worshipers.
