# William Henry Monk

William Henry Monk was born in London in 1823. A lecturer, composer, editor, and professor of vocal music at King's College, London, he received a Doctor of Music degree and became one of the most influential figures in Victorian church music. He died in 1889.

## The Tune "Hursley"

Monk's most beloved contribution to congregational song is "Hursley," arranged from a German chorale by Peter Ritter (1760–1846) and fitted to [[John_Keble]]'s evening hymn "Sun of My Soul, My Saviour Dear." Butterworth-Brown describe it as "a choice example of polyphonal sweetness in uniform long notes of perfect chord" and note that it superseded an earlier tune, "Halle," by [[Thomas_Hastings]].

## Hymns Ancient and Modern

Monk served as the musical editor of *Hymns Ancient and Modern*, the landmark Anglican hymnal first published in 1861 whose influence on English congregational song was immeasurable. His editorial work shaped the musical taste of an entire generation of English churchgoers, and his taste ran consistently to the polished and the dignified.

Monk belongs to the same tradition as [[John_B_Dykes]] — both were Victorian Anglicans who brought genuine musical scholarship to the service of congregational worship, and both shaped the sound of *Hymns Ancient and Modern* in its formative years.
