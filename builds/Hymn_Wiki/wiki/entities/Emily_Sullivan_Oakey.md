# Emily Sullivan Oakey

Emily Sullivan Oakey spent her professional life as a teacher and her creative life as a poet, and one poem from that creative life found its way into the gospel meeting and stayed there.

## Life

Oakey was born in Albany, New York, on October 8, 1829, and died on May 11, 1883. She taught languages at the Albany Female Academy, bringing to her students the disciplines of Latin, French, and modern literature. Her literary work ran alongside her teaching throughout her career.

## "What Shall the Harvest Be?"

Written in 1850, this hymn of evangelistic appeal draws on the agricultural metaphor of sowing and harvest --- one of the oldest and most natural in Christian preaching. Its question is the question of the revival meeting and the Judgment: what will be the fruit of this life, this prayer, this moment?

The hymn carries a musical shape well suited to gospel meetings: a verse that builds and a chorus that lands the question firmly. It became part of the repertoire of mid-century American revivalism and appeared in gospel song collections of the period.
