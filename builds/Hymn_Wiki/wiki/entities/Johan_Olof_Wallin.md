# Johan Olof Wallin

When Longfellow translated Tegner's Swedish poem "Children of the Lord's Supper," he introduced Johan Olof Wallin to the English-speaking world in these lines:

> And with one voice
> Chimed in the congregation, and sang an anthem immortal
> Of the sublime Wallin, of David's harp in the Northland.

Wallin is Scandinavia's greatest hymnist and perhaps the foremost in the entire Christian Church during the nineteenth century. That is a large claim. The Swedish Psalm-book of 1819 is the evidence behind it.

## David's Harp in the Northland

The Swedish Psalm-book of 1819 has been the hymn-book of the Swedish people for more than a century, used in the homeland and wherever Swedes have settled throughout the world. Of the 500 hymns in this volume:

- 128 are original hymns from Wallin's pen
- 178 are his revisions of other hymns
- 23 are his translations
- 13 are semi-originals based on other authors' work

In brief: 342 of the 500 hymns reflect the genius of this one man.

## The Making of an Archbishop

Wallin was born in 1779 at Stora Tuna, in the Dalarne province of Sweden. He overcame the handicaps of poverty and poor health, and by the age of twenty-four had earned a Doctor of Philosophy at Uppsala University. In 1805 and again in 1809, he won the chief prize for poetry at Uppsala.

He was ordained to the Lutheran ministry in 1806. Very soon he attracted attention by his preaching. In 1812 he was transferred to Stockholm; in 1816 he became Dean of Västerås; in 1824 he was elevated to the bishopric; and in 1837 he became Archbishop of Uppsala --- the Primate of the Church of Sweden. He died in 1839.

## The Psalm-Book

As early as 1807 Wallin had begun publishing collections of old and new hymns. In 1811 the Swedish parliament appointed a commission to prepare a new hymn-book. Wallin was made a member. The commission's first draft did not satisfy him, and eventually the entire task of completing the Psalm-book was charged to him alone. On November 28, 1816, he was able to report that he had finished his labors. The new hymn-book was officially authorized by King Karl XIV on January 29, 1819. It has remained in use to the present day.

Wallin held an uncompromising standard for what a hymn should be. He declared: *"A new hymn, aside from the spiritual considerations which should never be compromised in any way, should be so correct, simple and lyrical in form, and so free from inversions and other imperfections in style, that after the lapse of a hundred years a father may be able to say to his son, 'Read the Psalm-book, my boy, and you will learn your mother tongue!'"*

The profound influence of his hymns on the Swedish language for more than a century testifies to the faithfulness with which he adhered to that standard.

## Faith Found Through Labor

Some have charged that Wallin's earlier hymns were tinged by rationalism, and it is true that in his younger years he was influenced by the "New Theology" sweeping Europe. But as he became more absorbed in the great task of the Psalm-book, his own spiritual life deepened.

The turning point came publicly in 1816, when he declared war on rationalism before the Swedish Bible Society. The atonement of Christ now became the central theme in his hymn-book. The pure evangelical tone rings clearly in one of his own hymns:

> There is a truth so dear to me,
> I'll hold it fast eternally,
> It is my soul's chief treasure:
> That Jesus for the world hath died,
> He for my sins was crucified ---
> O love beyond all measure!

And faith's certainty in the lines that have become dear to the Swedish people:

> Blessed, blessed he who knoweth
>   That his faith on Thee is founded,
> Whom the Father's love bestoweth
>   Of eternal grace unbounded...

## The Christmas Hymn

Wallin's Christmas hymn "All hail to thee, O blessed morn" has been sung in every sanctuary in Sweden as a greeting to the dawn of Christmas day for more than a century.

> All hail to thee, O blessed morn!
> To tidings long by prophets borne
>   Hast thou fulfilment given.
> O sacred and immortal day,
> When unto earth, in glorious ray,
>   Descends the grace of heaven!

## Legacy

Wallin is a unique figure: a man whose life's work was not composing hymns in the usual sense, but shaping an entire national hymn-book. He translated, revised, and created until 342 of 500 hymns bore his touch. He held his own language and his Church's worship to the highest standard. And under the pressure of that great labor, he found his own faith deepening.

Longfellow's phrase remains the truest: David's harp in the Northland.
