# A.J. Gordon

The Rev. Adoniram Judson Gordon, D.D., was born in New Hampton, New Hampshire, on April 19, 1836. He became pastor of the Clarendon Street Baptist Church in Boston, where he served with what Butterworth-Brown call "unsurpassed usefulness to his fellowmen and devotion to his Divine Master." He died in Boston on February 2, 1895. A descendant of John Robinson of Leyden, he is remembered not only for his ministry but for the institutions that grew from it — what became Gordon College and Gordon-Conwell Theological Seminary.

Butterworth-Brown compare him to Phillips Brooks: both went to their graves "in all their glorious prime," and his loss was "equally lamented."

## The Coronation Hymnal

Gordon was the originating spirit behind the *Coronation Hymnal*, a collection into whose making went five years of prayer and planning. While the volume was slowly taking form, he was wont to "hum to himself, or cause to be played by one of his family, snatches and suggestions of new airs that came to him in connection with his own hymns, and others which seemed to have no suitable music."

## My Jesus, I Love Thee

The anonymous hymn "My Jesus, I Love Thee" presented such a challenge. Gordon found it in a London hymn-book; the English tune sent to him later "did not sound sympathetic." Dissatisfied, and with a clear ideal in his mind of what the feeling should be, "he meditated and prayed over the words till in a moment of inspiration the beautiful air sang itself to him" — with its simple concords, carrying the hymn "into the chapels of every denomination."

> My Jesus, I love Thee, I know Thou art mine,
> For Thee all the pleasures of sin I resign.

Butterworth-Brown record a memorable scene: passengers on a railway car en route to the Chicago World's Fair, tired and distracted, suddenly drawn into rapt attention as a young husband and wife began singing this tune in clear tenor and soprano. "A reverential hush had been cast over the audience by the sacred music" — no applause, only a gentle request for more.

## Other Musical Contributions

Gordon composed four tunes in the *Coronation Hymnal*, three set to his own words. His hymn "O Holy Ghost, Arise" is "still sung, and called for affectionately as 'Gordon's Hymn.'"

## Baptist Tradition Context (Burrage)

Burrage confirms the outline of Gordon's Baptist career. He was born in New Hampton, New Hampshire, on April 19, 1836, converted in early life, and entered Brown University with the Christian ministry in view, graduating in 1860. He completed theological study at Newton Theological Institution in 1863. His ministry began at Jamaica Plain, Massachusetts (ordained June 29, 1863) and resulted in large additions to that church. He was then called to Clarendon Street Baptist Church, Boston, as successor to Rev. Baron Stow, D.D., where Burrage writes that he labored "preaching the gospel with great freshness and spiritual power, and exerting a wide influence in behalf of evangelical religion."

Burrage specifically notes Gordon's partnership with D. L. Moody: "During his work in Boston, Mr. Moody found in Dr. Gordon one of his strongest helpers, and he seeks his assistance whenever the Boston pastor is within reach."

Gordon compiled "Service of Song" (1871) and wrote several notable books: "In Christ, or the Believer's Union with his Lord" (1872), "Congregational Worship" (1872), "Grace and Glory" (1880), "Ministry of Healing" (1882), "The Two-Fold Life" (1883), and edited the monthly publication "The Watchword" from 1877. Brown University conferred the D.D. on him in 1878.

Burrage preserves a hymn Gordon wrote in the summer of 1886 at the Northfield School for Bible Study — written at the request of college students who had committed to foreign missions and were going out to visit colleges to awaken missionary interest. The hymn, "Whom Shall I Send?" (based on Isaiah 6:8), carried their message of missionary urgency from campus to campus.
