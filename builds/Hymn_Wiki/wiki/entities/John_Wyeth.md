# John Wyeth

John Wyeth was a printer and publisher in Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, who compiled two of the most influential sacred music books of the early nineteenth century. His *Repository of Sacred Music* (1810) and its supplement became important sources for the rural folk hymnody that flowed into the shape-note tradition.

## Life

Wyeth was born in 1770 and lived to the exceptional age of eighty-eight, dying in 1858. He was primarily a printer and businessman in Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, where he established himself in the book and newspaper trade. He was not a professional musician, but his editorial eye and access to a printing press made him a significant figure in the dissemination of sacred music.

## Musical Work

His *Repository of Sacred Music* (1810) gathered a wide range of psalm tunes and hymn tunes, including many of American origin, and presented them in a format accessible to rural congregations and singing schools. His *Repository of Sacred Music, Part Second* (1813) brought in a larger proportion of folk-derived melodies and has been credited as an important source for the camp-meeting and shape-note traditions. The tunes in these books circulated widely and influenced later compilers throughout the South and West.

According to Metcalf, Wyeth's collections helped bridge the gap between the more formal New England church music tradition and the looser, more experiential hymnody of the frontier.
