# Edmund S. Lorenz

Edmund Simon Lorenz (1854–1942) was an American hymn writer, music publisher, and scholar of hymnology. He held both an LL.D. and a Mus. Doc., reflecting his dual expertise in letters and music. Lorenz was the author of several influential works on church music, including *Music in Work and Worship*, *Practical Hymn Studies*, *Practical Church Music*, and *Church Music*.

## Life and Career

Lorenz was a prominent figure in American Methodist church music during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. He founded the Lorenz Publishing Company, which became one of the leading publishers of church music in America. His practical orientation — combining scholarly knowledge with pastoral concern — distinguished him from purely academic hymnologists.

## The Singing Church (1938)

His most comprehensive work, *[[The_Singing_Church|The Singing Church: The Hymns It Wrote and Sang]]*, published by Cokesbury Press in Nashville, synthesizes the history of Christian hymnody from the apostolic era through the early 20th century. Lorenz explicitly aimed to be "pre-eminently helpful" rather than "pre-eminently scholarly," writing for pastors and church leaders who needed practical guidance alongside historical understanding.

The work is notable for:
- Defending the [[Gospel_Hymn|Gospel hymn]] against literary critics
- Articulating the Watts (objective) vs. Wesley (subjective) framework
- Documenting extensive hymn emendation history
- Providing practical pastoral guidance on hymn selection and use

## Legacy

Through his publishing company and his writings, Lorenz influenced how American Protestant congregations understood and practiced congregational singing well into the 20th century.
