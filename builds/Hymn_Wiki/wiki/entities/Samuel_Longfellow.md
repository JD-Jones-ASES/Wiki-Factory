# Samuel Longfellow

Samuel Longfellow (1819–1892) was an American Unitarian minister and hymn writer, the younger brother of the famous poet Henry Wadsworth Longfellow.

## Life and Career

Longfellow was born in Portland, Maine, June 18, 1819. He graduated at Harvard in 1839 and at the Cambridge Divinity School in 1846. In 1848 he became pastor of the Second Unitarian Church in Brooklyn, New York, where he remained until 1860. After that he did not serve as a regular pastor but preached occasionally and engaged in a variety of literary labor. He died at Portland, Maine, October 3, 1892.

In connection with Rev. Samuel Johnson, he compiled A Book of Hymns (1846) and Hymns of the Spirit (1864).

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

Three of Longfellow's hymns appear in the Methodist Hymnal:

- "Again as evening's shadow falls"
- "I look to thee in every need"
- "O still in accents sweet and strong"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett note Longfellow's collaboration with Samuel Johnson on two significant mid-century Unitarian hymnals. Prof. F. M. Bird is quoted as saying that Johnson's contributions to these collections "were less numerous but not less meritorious than those by Mr. Longfellow," suggesting Longfellow was the dominant hymnist of the partnership. Like [[Frederick_Lucian_Hosmer]], Longfellow's Unitarian hymns crossed denominational boundaries to find use in Methodist worship.
