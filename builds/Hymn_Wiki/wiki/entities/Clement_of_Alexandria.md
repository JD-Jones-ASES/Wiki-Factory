# Clement of Alexandria

Clement of Alexandria holds a unique place in the history of Christian worship: he is credited with writing --- or at least preserving --- what may be the oldest Christian hymn still known to us, "Shepherd of Tender Youth."

## Who He Was

His full name was Titus Flavius Clemens, and he was born around 150 AD. Before he became a Christian, Clement was a serious intellectual --- the kind of person who couldn't stop asking questions. He traveled widely, studying the religious and philosophical traditions of the Greeks, Assyrians, Egyptians, and Jews, always searching for truth.

Eventually he found his way to Alexandria, Egypt, where a Christian scholar named Pantaenus was running something remarkable: the first Christian school of its kind in the world, a place where Greek philosophy and Christian faith were brought into serious conversation. Clement enrolled as a student --- and was so transformed by what he found there that he eventually became the school's director himself.

He was, according to Ryden, one of the first great scholars in the Christian Church. Among the students who sat under his teaching was the brilliant [[Origen]], who would go on to become the most celebrated scholar in the ancient Christian world.

## The Hymn

Clement's most celebrated work was a three-volume book called *The Tutor*, which used Christ as the model tutor for the Christian life. Appended to this book was a poem titled "A Hymn to the Saviour" --- a children's hymn addressed to Christ as the shepherd of young souls. Its opening stanza reads:

> Shepherd of tender youth,
> Guiding in love and truth
> Through devious ways;
> Christ, our triumphant King,
> We come Thy Name to sing,
> And here our children bring
> To join Thy praise.

According to Ryden, all surviving manuscripts attribute this hymn to Clement himself, though some scholars have suggested he may have been quoting an even earlier writer. Either way, the hymn has always been known as "Clement's hymn," and "aside from the hymns derived from the Bible, it is the oldest Christian hymn in existence."

It is a hymn of extraordinary charm --- childlike in its simplicity, yet rich in its theology. Christ is shepherd, king, healer, guiding word, and the source of life, all woven together in four brief stanzas.

## Persecution and Death

In 202 AD, the Emperor Severus launched a wave of persecution against Christians. Clement was driven out of Alexandria and forced to flee. Very little is known about the remaining years of his life. He is believed to have died around 220 AD, never returning to the city where he had done his greatest work.

## Significance

Clement stands at the beginning of the Christian hymn tradition. He represents the point where Christian worship moved beyond the Psalms and Scriptural canticles into the realm of original sacred poetry. His hymn has been sung in some form for more than eighteen centuries --- a remarkable witness to the staying power of simple, sincere praise.

He also models something important for the history of [[Early_Christian_Hymnody]]: that Christian worship and intellectual life belong together. His school in Alexandria was a place where faith was not afraid of questions, and his hymn shows that the life of the mind and the song of the heart can inhabit the same moment.
