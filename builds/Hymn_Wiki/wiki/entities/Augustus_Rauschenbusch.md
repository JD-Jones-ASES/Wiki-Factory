# Augustus Rauschenbusch

Augustus Rauschenbusch was born in Altena, Westphalia, Germany, February 13, 1816. His father, who gave him careful early instruction, was pastor of the Lutheran church in that city. At fourteen he entered the gymnasium at Elberfeld; four years later, the theological department of the University of Berlin, where under the influence of Neander and other pious friends he was led to a saving knowledge of the truth. He later studied natural science and theology at the University of Bonn.

When his father died in 1841, he was made his father's successor at Altena, where his earnest evangelical ministry was greatly blessed. But he was not at ease under the restrictions of a state church position, and in 1846 he crossed the Atlantic to labor among his German countrymen in the United States. He preached a short time in Missouri, then in 1847 was placed in charge of the German tract department of the American Tract Society in New York.

## Conversion to Baptist Views

While at the American Tract Society, Rauschenbusch was led to examine the question of baptism. The result of his independent investigation was that he accepted Baptist views, and was baptized in May 1850 — by Sigismund Kupfer, according to Burrage. In 1851 he labored in Canada and organized the first German Baptist churches there, while still connected with the American Tract Society. He severed that connection in 1853.

Returning to the United States with a party of German emigrants in 1854, he settled with them in Missouri and organized a German Baptist church in Gasconade in 1855. That same year, at the request of the New York Baptist Union, he organized the German Department of the Theological Seminary in Rochester, New York, receiving a professorship he held with "great acceptance" until the summer of 1888 — when, in ill health, he resigned and returned to Germany.

Burrage closes his account with a warmly personal benediction: "May his last days, amid the scenes of his youth, be crowned with abundant blessings!"

## His Hymnbook and Hymns

Rauschenbusch compiled the *Pilgerharfe*, a collection of hymns for use in the German Baptist churches. He also wrote a learned tract on the Lord's Day, published by the American Tract Society in English and German. One of his baptismal hymns, number 324 in the *Pilgerharfe*, begins:

*In des Jordans kühle Wellen / Stieg der Heiland einst hinab* ("Once where flows the sacred Jordan / Christ was buried 'neath the wave").

## Family Note

According to Burrage, [[Walther_Rauschenbusch]] — the later Baptist minister and Social Gospel theologian — is the son of Augustus Rauschenbusch, born in America while his father was engaged in his Rochester professorship.
