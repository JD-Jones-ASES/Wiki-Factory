# Elizabeth Payson Prentiss

Elizabeth Payson Prentiss (1818–1878) was an American author and hymn writer, daughter of the famous revivalist preacher Edward Payson of Portland, Maine, and wife of Rev. George L. Prentiss, D.D., an eminent Presbyterian divine and professor in Union Theological Seminary of New York City.

## Life and Career

Prentiss was born at Portland, Maine, on October 28, 1818. She became a contributor of both prose and poetry to the Youth's Companion as early as her sixteenth year. She was a devotedly pious woman. She taught school in Portland, in Ipswich, Massachusetts, and in Richmond, Virginia. She was married in 1845 to Rev. George L. Prentiss.

She was never in robust health, but did much literary work, publishing several volumes. Her Stepping Heavenward (1869) is one of the most popular books ever published in the English language. Her Religious Poems appeared in 1873, and her Golden Hours; or, Hymns and Songs of the Christian Life, in 1874. She died August 13, 1878. Soon after her death her husband published her Life and Letters.

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

- "More love to thee, O Christ"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

According to Nutter and Tillett, Prentiss's hymn "More love to thee, O Christ" was a natural expression of a faith that had been formed through physical suffering and grief. Her best-known book, Stepping Heavenward, was a fictional diary of a young woman's spiritual growth — enormously popular in the late nineteenth century — and the same themes of consecration and longing for deeper love of Christ run through her hymns.
