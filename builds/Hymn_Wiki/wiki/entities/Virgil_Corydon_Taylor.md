# Virgil Corydon Taylor

Virgil Corydon Taylor was a dedicated music educator whose entire life was devoted to bringing choral music and organ culture to the churches and communities of the northeastern United States.

## Life

Taylor was born in Barkhamsted, Connecticut, on April 2, 1817. His father bore the same name, and the family could trace its ancestry back through female lines to Elder William Brewster, the Pilgrim who drew up the Mayflower Compact in 1620.

In his Connecticut home, his father had placed a small instrument called a "church organ," and on this Taylor composed many of his early pieces. He spent his career teaching singing schools, holding musical institutes, and serving as an organist and private teacher. He relocated to Hartford for a time, then moved to Poughkeepsie, New York, in 1851 to lead the Poughkeepsie Union Musical Association. He died in 1891.

## Music

Taylor was a competent composer who contributed tunes to several mid-century collections. Like many musicians of his generation, his work was more widely known in his own lifetime than it has proven in the long run. His real contribution was as an educator and organizer of musical life in communities that might otherwise have had little access to formal music instruction.
