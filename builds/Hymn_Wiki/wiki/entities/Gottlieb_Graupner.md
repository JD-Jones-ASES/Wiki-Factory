# Gottlieb Graupner

Gottlieb Graupner was the organizational backbone of early Boston musical life. A German immigrant with real musical ability and business sense, he helped create the institutions --- including the Handel and Haydn Society --- that became the foundation of American classical and sacred music.

## Life and Career

Graupner was born in 1767 in Germany. He came to America and settled in Boston, where he became a music publisher and teacher. He is prominently associated with the founding of the Handel and Haydn Society, which became the most important choral organization in early America and the body under whose name Lowell Mason's first great collection was published.

At concerts in Boston and Salem, Graupner led the orchestra while Dr. George K. Jackson directed from the organ. A contemporary diary entry by Dr. William Bentley of Salem (December 1, 1812) noted: "Mr. Graupner led the violins, Mrs. Graupner was at the head of the female singers, which were seven in number." (Metcalf, p. 48)

He operated a music store in Boston and was in partnership for a time with Mallett, a French musician. His music publishing activities made him a key conduit for bringing European sacred music to American churches and concert halls.

Graupner died in 1836. His direct musical legacy is modest --- few of his compositions remained in use --- but his role as an institution-builder and his connection to the Handel and Haydn Society place him among the founders of American musical culture.
