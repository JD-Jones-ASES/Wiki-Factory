# Morgan Rhys

Morgan Rhys belonged to the inner circle of the Welsh evangelical revival of the eighteenth century --- a schoolmaster and preacher who shared that world with Howel Harris, Daniel Rowland, and above all with William Williams of Pantycelyn, whose friendship and influence shaped his work.

## Life

Rhys was born in 1716 and died in 1779. He worked as a schoolmaster as well as a preacher, a combination common among the early Welsh Methodists who saw education and evangelism as complementary callings. His connection to Williams of Pantycelyn was personal as well as theological: they moved in the same circles, attended the same Association meetings, and shaped each other's understanding of what Welsh evangelical hymnody should be.

## "Lo! A Saviour for the Fallen"

This hymn proclaims the gospel in the direct, declarative mode characteristic of Welsh revival preaching: not argument but announcement. The Saviour is for the fallen --- not for the righteous, not for the respectable, not for those who have earned his attention, but for those who have fallen and know it. The hymn belongs to the Calvinistic Methodist tradition of grace as the last and only resort of ruined souls.
