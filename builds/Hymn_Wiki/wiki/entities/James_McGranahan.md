# James McGranahan

James McGranahan inherited the mantle of Philip Paul Bliss and wore it with distinction. When Bliss died in the Ashtabula train disaster of 1876, the evangelistic work he had shared with Major D.W. Whittle needed a new musical partner. McGranahan became that partner, and through his collaboration with Whittle, Sankey, and Stebbins, he shaped the sound of a generation of gospel meetings.

## Life and Calling

McGranahan was born on July 4, 1840, in Adamsville, Crawford County, Pennsylvania. He trained as a musician and built his reputation as a soloist and song leader before entering full-time evangelistic work. His connection to Bliss gave him a direct link to the founding tradition of the Gospel Hymns movement.

After Bliss's death, McGranahan joined Major Whittle's evangelistic campaigns as the resident singer and composer, filling the same role Bliss had occupied --- writing new tunes, leading congregational singing, and setting Whittle's revival addresses to music.

## Gospel Hymns

McGranahan became one of the three principal co-editors of the later volumes in the *Gospel Hymns* series alongside [[Ira_Sankey]] and [[George_Coles_Stebbins]]. This collaboration produced the most widely distributed gospel music of the era. The *Gospel Hymns* series sold millions of copies and defined what evangelical congregations sang in the last decades of the nineteenth century.

## His Music

McGranahan's composing style leaned toward confident, forward-moving tunes that suited the evangelistic meeting. Among the pieces associated with him are tunes for "He Will Hide Me," the urgent call of "Come," and the revival plea "Revive Thy Work, O Lord." His settings consistently matched the emotional register of the words they carried --- warm enough for personal faith, vigorous enough for a crowded hall.
