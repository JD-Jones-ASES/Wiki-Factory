# Martin Madan

Rev. Martin Madan, son of Col. Madan, was born in 1726. He founded Lock Hospital in Hyde Park and long officiated as its chaplain. As a preacher he was popular, and his reputation as a composer of music was considerable. There is no proof that he wrote any original hymns, but he amended, pieced, and expanded the work of others. He died in 1770.

## Editor and Reviser

Madan's chief contribution to hymnody was his work as an editor. He revised and adapted [[William_Hammond]]'s hymn "Awake and Sing the Song of Moses and the Lamb" into the form still used, and his work on various other hymns helped give them the more polished versions that survived into later hymnals.

His anthem "Denmark" — a four-page florid choral — was set to Isaac Watts's great hymn "Before Jehovah's Awful Throne." Butterworth-Brown describe it as having "some grand strains" but note that it is "a tune of florid and difficult vocalization," now heard only in Old Folks' Concerts.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Madan represents a type of 18th-century clerical musician — the preacher-composer who shaped the practical worship materials of the evangelical movement without producing the great original hymns himself. His Lock Hospital chapel was one of the centers of evangelical musical life in London, and his willingness to take rough materials and refine them into singable form was a genuine service to the movement. His reputation as a composer and his associations with the Wesleys and Whitefield placed him at the intersection of the great evangelical musical world of his era.
