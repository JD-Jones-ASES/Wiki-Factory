# Washington Gladden

Washington Gladden (1836–1918) was a distinguished American Congregationalist minister, author, and social reformer, one of the most widely known and influential pastors and religious writers of his day.

## Life and Career

Gladden was the son of Solomon Gladden and was born at Pottsgrove, Pennsylvania, February 11, 1836. Reared on a farm near Oswego, New York, and educated in a country district school and at Oswego Academy, he first learned the printer's trade and later entered Williams College, from which he graduated in 1859. He was licensed to preach in 1860.

He served as pastor of Congregational Churches in Brooklyn, NY (1860); Morrisania, NY (1861–66); North Adams, Mass. (1866–71); Springfield, Mass. (1875–82); and from 1882 to 1911 he was pastor of the First Congregational Church of Columbus, Ohio. From 1871 to 1875 he was on the editorial staff of the New York Independent, and later edited the weekly periodical Sunday Afternoon.

## Social and Literary Work

In deep sympathy with the masses and the working people, his voice and pen were long exercised in the work of social reform. According to Nutter and Tillett, he was the author of about thirty widely read volumes on religious, ethical, and social subjects, including:

- Plain Thoughts on the Art of Living (1868)
- Workingmen and Their Employers (1876)
- The Young Men and the Churches (1885)
- Applied Christianity (1887)
- Who Wrote the Bible? (1891)
- The Church and the Kingdom (1894)
- Ruling Ideas of the Present Age (1895)
- The Christian Pastor (1898)
- Social Salvation (1901)
- Christianity and Socialism (1905)
- Recollections (1909)

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

Gladden contributed one hymn to the Methodist Hymnal:

- "O Master, let me walk with thee"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett present Gladden as a figure whose fame as a preacher, lecturer, and social reformer overshadowed his hymnic contribution, but "O Master, let me walk with thee" proved enduringly popular across denominations. It captures Gladden's characteristic emphasis on practical discipleship and Christian engagement with the world — themes that ran throughout all his work.
