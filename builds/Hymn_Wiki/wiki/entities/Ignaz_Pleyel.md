# Ignaz Pleyel

Ignaz Joseph Pleyel was born in 1757 — the twenty-fourth child of a village schoolmaster in Austria. The remarkable fact of his birth order is noted by Butterworth-Brown as characteristic of the unlikely origins from which great musical talent sometimes springs. He studied under [[Joseph_Haydn]] — the greatest living composer — and became famous across Europe as a musician and composer. He established in Paris the Pleyel piano manufacturing firm, which bore his name for generations. He died in 1831.

## The Tune "Brattle Street"

Pleyel's contribution to congregational music is the tune "Brattle Street," composed for [[Helen_Maria_Williams]]'s hymn "While Thee I Seek, Protecting Power." Butterworth-Brown describe it as an "emotional choral" so closely associated with Williams's text that "few lovers of the hymn recall its words without the melody."

The name "Brattle Street" is an American designation — Brattle Street in Cambridge, Massachusetts, being a notable address in New England church life. The tune's gentle, flowing character suits Williams's intimate prayer of devotion and trust.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Pleyel represents a type common in hymn history: the distinguished secular musician whose work found a secondary life in the church. His fame in his own time rested on his piano music and his Parisian firm; his lasting contribution to the devotional life of English-speaking congregations is a single chorale, still warming the room whenever Williams's hymn is sung.
