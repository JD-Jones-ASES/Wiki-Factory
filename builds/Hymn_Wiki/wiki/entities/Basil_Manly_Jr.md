# Basil Manly Jr.

Among the Baptists of the South the name of Manly is very intimately associated with Christian song. Basil Manly Jr. was the son of Dr. Basil Manly of South Carolina — a prominent figure in Southern Baptist life — and he carried both the family's love of hymns and the family's theological rigor into his own distinguished career.

## Life

Manly was born in Edgefield County, South Carolina, December 19, 1825. He fitted for college at a preparatory school in Charleston, then entered the University of Alabama at Tuscaloosa, graduating in 1843. He then entered Newton Theological Institution, and subsequently Princeton Theological Seminary, graduating in 1847. He was ordained at Tuscaloosa, Alabama, in 1848, and commenced his ministry as pastor of three country churches (two in Sumter County, Alabama, and one in Noxubee County, Mississippi). The manifold labors of these widely separated pastorates impaired his health, and he withdrew from active ministry until September 1850.

He then accepted a call to the pastorate of the First Baptist church in Richmond, Virginia. In 1854, his health again failed; he resigned and established the Richmond Female Institute, of which he became principal. When the Southern Baptist Theological Seminary was organized at Greenville, South Carolina, in 1859, Manly was appointed professor of biblical interpretation. During the Civil War the seminary was suspended; when it reopened he resumed his professorship. In 1871 he accepted the presidency of Georgetown College, Kentucky, serving until 1879, when he was elected professor of Old Testament interpretation in the Southern Baptist Theological Seminary, now relocated to Louisville.

The University of Alabama conferred the D.D. on him in 1859; the Agricultural College at Auburn, Alabama, conferred the LL.D. in 1874. His scholarly reputation was widely acknowledged. His published works include *A Call to the Ministry* (1867) and *The Bible Doctrine of Inspiration Explained and Vindicated* (1888, described by Burrage as "a work of great excellence").

## Baptist Psalmody

In 1849, while withdrawn from active ministry, Manly compiled — with the aid of his father — the *Baptist Psalmody*, published in Charleston in 1850. This collection had an extensive circulation in the southern states and is described by Burrage as "one of great excellence."

Manly contributed nine original hymns:

- "Holy, holy, holy Lord"
- "Jesus, my Lord, I own thee God"
- "God with us, O glorious name"
- "Our God invites the wanderers home"
- "Lord, I deserve thy deepest wrath"
- "Before the pool a sufferer lay"
- "In doubt's dim twilight here I stray"
- "God of the seas, whose ruling voice"
- "There is a light which shines from heaven"

He has also written twenty or thirty additional hymns, some of which have found their way into various collections.

## Seminary Hymn

The following hymn was written by Manly at the request of Dr. James P. Boyce, for the first commencement of the Southern Baptist Theological Seminary at Greenville in 1860, and has been sung at every commencement of the seminary since:

*Soldiers of Christ, in truth arrayed, / A world in ruins needs your aid, / A world by sin destroyed and dead, / A world for which the Savior bled.*

*We meet to part, but part to meet, / When earthly labors are complete, / To join in yet more blest employ / In an eternal world of joy.*

## Baptist Tradition Context (Burrage)

Manly's career spans the most turbulent period in American Baptist history: the division between Northern and Southern Baptists in 1845 over slavery, the Civil War itself, and the rebuilding of Southern Baptist institutions afterward. His work on the *Baptist Psalmody* and his contributions to seminary hymnody reflect the Southern Baptist determination to build a robust and self-sufficient denominational culture — including its own theological education, its own publications, and its own song.

Burrage includes a portrait of Manly as one of only five illustrations in the entire book — a measure of his standing in American Baptist life in 1888.
