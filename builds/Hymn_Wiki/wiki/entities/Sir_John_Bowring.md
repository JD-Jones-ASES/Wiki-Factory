# Sir John Bowring

Sir John Bowring was one of the most remarkable men of the nineteenth century — a figure who combined immense practical power with genuine scholarly breadth and sincere religious feeling. Butterworth-Brown describe him as having "impressed himself perhaps more than any other save Napoleon Bonaparte upon his own generation." He was born in 1792 and died in 1872.

A linguist of extraordinary capacity, Bowring was said to know more than a hundred languages. He served as a Member of Parliament, and as Governor of Hong Kong, he was one of the most influential British administrators in Asia. He was also a devoted man of faith whose religious convictions did not sit easily with his public career's compromises but found their fullest expression in hymn.

## In the Cross of Christ I Glory

Bowring's great hymn of personal faith is built on a single controlling image — the Cross as the Christian's only glory, the world's only true light:

> In the Cross of Christ I glory,
>   Towering o'er the wrecks of time.

It is a hymn that transcends its Unitarian author's particular theology by the universality of its devotional posture. Butterworth-Brown note the tune associated with it is "Rathbun," composed by Ithamar Conkey, a bass singer.

## Watchman Tell Us of the Night

This missionary-dialogue hymn — cast as an antiphonal exchange between a watchman and a traveler on a dark mountain road — is described by Butterworth-Brown as ranking "with the foremost missionary lyrics." The tune written by [[Lowell_Mason]] for it was never superseded:

> Watchman, tell us of the night,
>   What its signs of promise are!

The piece caught perfectly the spirit of the early nineteenth-century missionary movement: the waiting church, the pilgrim world, and the hope of dawn.

Lowell Mason's tune appears in his own tune-books under its first hymn-line and in a few hymnals as "Bowring" or "Watchman." Butterworth-Brown note that "in animation and vocal splendor it catches the author's own clear call."
