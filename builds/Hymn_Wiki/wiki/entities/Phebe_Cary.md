# Phebe Cary

Phebe Cary wrote one hymn that the entire century wanted at the bedside of the dying and the graveside of the dead. "One Sweetly Solemn Thought" was not planned for publication. It came quietly, in a borrowed room, and went out to do its work with equal quietness.

## Life

Cary was born near Cincinnati in 1824. She and her sister Alice Cary were part of the literary circles of mid-nineteenth-century America, known as poets and essayists of some reputation. Phebe died at Newport in 1871, at the age of forty-seven.

## "One Sweetly Solemn Thought"

The hymn was written in 1852 in a friend's back bedroom --- a detail that Butterworth-Brown preserve as evidence that great hymns come in ordinary circumstances. The poem meditates on the nearness of death and the soul's nearness to God: the thought that at any moment the spirit may be called to cross from this side of eternity to the other.

The hymn's power lies in its simplicity and its honesty. It does not declaim or theologize. It simply pauses at the threshold and looks across. That quality made it the natural choice for moments when words of elaborate comfort feel false and only the plain truth will do.
