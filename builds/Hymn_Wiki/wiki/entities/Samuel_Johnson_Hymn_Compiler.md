# Samuel Johnson (Hymn Compiler)

Samuel Johnson (1822–1882) was an American Unitarian minister, writer, and hymn compiler. Not to be confused with the English lexicographer Samuel Johnson (1709–1784), this Samuel Johnson was a colleague and close friend of [[Samuel_Longfellow]], with whom he compiled two important Unitarian hymnals.

## Life and Career

Johnson was born in Salem, Massachusetts, and graduated from Harvard Divinity School. He served briefly as a Unitarian minister but spent much of his career as an independent scholar and writer. He was known for his theological liberalism and his interest in comparative religion.

## Hymnal Compilations

In partnership with Samuel Longfellow, Johnson co-compiled:

1. **[[Book_of_Hymns_for_Public_and_Private_Devotion|A Book of Hymns for Public and Private Devotion]]** (1848) — 600 hymns for Unitarian worship, reaching a 15th edition by 1866
2. **Hymns of the Spirit** (1864) — A second, more theologically adventurous collection

Nutter and Tillett note that while Johnson's contributions were "less numerous" than Longfellow's, they were "not less meritorious," suggesting a complementary partnership where Longfellow was the more prolific hymnist and Johnson the more careful editor.

## Hymn Writing

Johnson contributed original hymns to both collections, though he is better remembered as an editor and compiler than as a hymn writer. His editorial standards helped shape the theological and literary character of these influential Unitarian hymnals.
