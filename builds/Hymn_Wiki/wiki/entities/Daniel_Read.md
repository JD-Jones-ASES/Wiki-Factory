# Daniel Read

Daniel Read was born November 16, 1757, in Rehoboth, Massachusetts, the son of Daniel and Mary Read. He served briefly as a soldier in 1777-1778 in three short expeditions into Rhode Island during the Revolutionary War — each lasting about a month. Before the close of the war he had moved to New Haven and entered a partnership with the engraver Amos Doolittle in book publishing and selling. About 1785 he married Jerusha Sherman. Their son was a Yale graduate (class of 1811) who became a clergyman; he died at sea near Cape Cod in August 1821.

## The Publisher-Composer

In addition to his book trade, Read manufactured ivory combs, held stock in a New Haven bank, served as a library director, and was active in public affairs. He corresponded with [[Oliver_Holden]] in 1793, subscribing to Holden's planned music publications. His portrait and that of Jerusha Read were eventually presented to the New Haven Colony Historical Society, which also holds a volume of manuscript music dated "Daniel Read. Saturday, July 9, 1777."

## Publications

*The American Singing Book* (1785) — printed in New Haven — was intended as a new and easy guide to the art of psalmody for singing schools. It had 73 pages and sold so well that a fourth edition appeared in January 1792. A supplement of 25 tunes from different composers was issued separately in 1787.

In 1786 Read began publishing *The American Musical Magazine* monthly, the first such periodical in America, containing "a great variety of approved music carefully selected from the works of the best American and European masters," published jointly with Doolittle.

*The Columbian Harmonist* (1793) reached its fourth edition in 1810 and contained three separately issued numbers, the third devoted to anthems and set pieces "suitable for Christmas, Good Friday, Easter, Fasts, Thanksgiving, Funerals." *An Introduction to Psalmody* appeared in 1790.

In 1817, at the request of the United Society of New Haven, he compiled a collection of music — *The New Haven Collection of Sacred Music* (1818), printed in Dedham by Daniel Mann. He bore the full labor of arranging and preparing it for the press.

His last work was completed in 1832 at age seventy-five: a manuscript of nearly three hundred pages containing over four hundred tunes, which he offered to the American Home Missionary Society for publication with proceeds to support missions. The Society declined the responsibility.

## The Tunes

Metcalf's survey found "Lisbon" and "Windham" in seven of the recent hymnals examined — a remarkable survival rate. "Sherburne" (a fugue tune) appeared in *The Methodist Hymnal* of 1878 alongside four other Read tunes. Charles Zeuner, in his *Ancient Lyre*, called "Windham" a choral by Martin Luther arranged by Read — a claim that speaks to the tune's German solidity. Metcalf noted that the survival of any of Read's compositions after more than a century "when we realize the change in sentiment regarding church music" was itself remarkable. (Metcalf, p. 95-99)

Daniel Read died in 1836 at age seventy-eight.
