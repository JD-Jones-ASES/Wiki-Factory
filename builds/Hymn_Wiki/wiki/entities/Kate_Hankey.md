# Kate Hankey

Kate Hankey wrote her two most famous hymns while she was lying ill. The circumstances were circumstances of necessity --- she was recovering from a serious illness in 1866 --- but what came from that sickroom entered the world singing, and has been singing ever since.

## Life

Arabella Catherine Hankey was born around 1834 into a prosperous English family; her father was a prominent London banker. She became deeply involved in evangelical Christian work, teaching Bible classes and engaging in philanthropic efforts, especially for young working women.

## The Convalescence Hymns

During her convalescence in 1866, Hankey wrote a long poem about the life of Christ. From this poem came two separable hymns, each of which acquired a life of its own.

"Tell Me the Old Old Story" expresses a soul's longing to hear again the simple facts of the gospel --- not argument, not theology, but the old story told plainly and tenderly. William H. Doane set it to a tune that captured exactly that tone of quiet pleading. The hymn found immediate acceptance and spread rapidly through the evangelical world.

"I Love to Tell the Story" came from a different section of the same long poem and takes a more active posture --- not the longing to hear but the joy of telling. Where "Tell Me" is contemplative and quiet, "I Love to Tell the Story" is outward-facing and warm. Both hymns together represent a full circle: the soul that receives the story and the soul that passes it on.

## Character

Hankey represents the English evangelical tradition at its most accessible. She was a practical Christian, not a theologian or a literary professional, and her hymns carry that quality --- the directness of someone who has heard something true and simply wants others to hear it too.
