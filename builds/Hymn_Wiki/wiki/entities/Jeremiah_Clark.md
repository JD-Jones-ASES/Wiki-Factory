# Jeremiah Clark

Jeremiah Clark was born in London in 1670. He received his musical education at the Chapel Royal, and went on to become organist of Winchester College and finally of St. Paul's Cathedral, where he was appointed Gentleman of the Chapel. He died in July 1707.

## The Tune "Nottingham" (St. Magnus)

Clark's tune "Nottingham" — now sometimes entitled "St. Magnus" — was composed for Thomas Sternhold's magnificent two-stanza versification of the 18th Psalm:

> The Lord descended from above
>   And bowed the heavens most high,
> And underneath His feet He cast
>   The darkness of the sky.
>
> On cherub and on cherubim
>   Full royally He rode,
> And on the wings of mighty winds
>   Came flying all abroad.

Butterworth-Brown describe "Nottingham" as "a dignified but spirited choral in A flat," a fitting echo of those grand verses. The tune belongs to the tradition of English cathedral psalmody at its finest — combining the solemnity of the older tradition with the harmonic refinement of the early 18th century.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Clark's musical career at St. Paul's placed him at the center of the Anglican choral tradition. His work represents the English school that [[Thomas_Tallis]] had founded and that Clark's own generation was developing into a distinctive voice of cathedral worship. The tune "Nottingham/St. Magnus" is one of the pieces that kept the best of 17th-century English sacred melody alive into the 18th-century hymnal tradition.
