# William Walsham How

William Walsham How (1823–1897) was a bishop of the Church of England and one of the most beloved English hymn writers of the nineteenth century, best known for "For all the saints, who from their labors rest."

## Life and Career

How was born at Shrewsbury, England, on December 13, 1823. He graduated Bachelor of Arts at Wadham College, Oxford, in 1845, and was ordained to the ministry in 1846. He held various positions in the Church of England before becoming bishop in 1888. He died August 10, 1897.

In connection with Rev. T. B. Morrell, he compiled a book of Psalms and Hymns in 1854. He contributed several hymns to Church Hymns in 1871. His sacred and secular pieces were collected and published in 1886 as Poems and Hymns.

## Character of His Hymns

According to Nutter and Tillett, Bishop How's hymns are characterized by a simplicity of manner and a warmth of feeling that have made some of them very popular. Six of them appear in the Methodist Hymnal:

- "For all the saints who from their labors rest"
- "Lord Jesus, when we stand afar"
- "O Jesus, crucified for man"
- "O Jesus, thou art standing"
- "O Word of God incarnate"
- "We give thee but thine own"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett emphasize How's accessibility and warmth as his defining virtues as a hymn writer. His hymns lack the elaborate theological architecture of some of his contemporaries, but compensate with directness of address and genuinely devotional feeling. The fact that six hymns reached the Methodist Hymnal speaks to his ecumenical appeal — his work transcended Anglican boundaries.
