# William Hammond

Rev. William Hammond was born on January 6, 1719, at Battle, Sussex, England, and educated at St. John's College, Cambridge. Early in his ministerial life he was a Calvinistic Methodist, but ultimately joined the Moravians. He died in London on August 19, 1793. His collection of *Psalms and Hymns and Spiritual Songs* was published in 1745.

## Awake and Sing the Song

Hammond's hymn "Awake and Sing the Song of Moses and the Lamb" was published in Lady Huntingdon's hymn-book and was much used in early Methodist revivals. The version now in use was slightly altered by Rev. [[Martin_Madan]]. A sixth stanza — a variation on a Watts hymn — was added in the *Brethren's Hymn-book* of 1801:

> There shall each heart and tongue
>   His endless praise proclaim,
> And sweeter voices join the song
>   Of Moses and the Lamb.

## Musical and Thematic Context

The hymn has had a variety of musical interpretations. The more modern piece is "St. Philip" by [[Edward_John_Hopkins]], Doctor of Music, born at Westminster, London, on June 30, 1818. Hopkins became organist of the Temple Church, London, and received his Doctor's degree from the Archbishop of Canterbury in 1882. His tune is described as an appropriate modern vehicle for Hammond's revival hymn.
