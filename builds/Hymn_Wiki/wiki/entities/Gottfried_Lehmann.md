# Gottfried Wilhelm Lehmann

Gottfried Wilhelm Lehmann was born in Hamburg, Germany, October 23, 1799. Soon after his birth his parents removed to Berlin. He went to Leer, in East Friesland, to learn the saddler's trade, but returned to Berlin and became an engraver and lithographer. While in East Friesland he was drawn to the Christian faith, and after his return to Berlin joined a circle of believers interested in missions, Bible distribution, and temperance work.

## Connection to John G. Oncken

To procure Bibles at low cost, Lehmann applied to J. G. Oncken of Hamburg, then an agent of the Edinburgh Bible Society. This chance contact in 1830 proved to be the most important of his life. Oncken was baptized by Dr. Sears at Hamburg, April 22, 1834. Lehmann, who had by independent study already been convinced of the necessity of believers' baptism, was baptized with six others on May 14, 1837, by Oncken, in a lake near Berlin. On the following day this little congregation was organized as a Baptist church, and Lehmann was appointed pastor — while still continuing his business.

The early years brought "almost unsurmountable difficulties and severe persecutions." In 1838, Lehmann entered the service of the American Baptist Missionary Union. In 1840, he went to England, where he was ordained at Salter's Hall Chapel, Cannon Street, London, on June 29.

The revolution of 1848 brought the German Baptists in Prussia full religious liberty. Lehmann made many extensive missionary tours into eastern Prussia, visited England again to raise funds for a chapel in Berlin, and through his labors the Berlin Baptists secured a permanent home that became headquarters for wide-spread activities.

He was one of the founders of the Berlin branch of the Evangelical Alliance. Though stricken by disease several times, he lived to see the Baptist church in Berlin receive the rights of incorporation. He died February 21, 1882.

## His Hymns

Burrage notes that Lehmann possessed the gift of leadership, and with Oncken and Kobner guided the Baptist movement in Germany for many years. He was also the author of several hymns, including these preserved in *Die Glaubensharfe*:

- "O welche grosse Friedens-Schaar"
- "Wenn Zions Weg verlasst ein Herz"
- "Du versankst in das Grab"
- "Glaubig tauch' ich nieder"
- "Heil! uns vereint die Jesuslieb'"
- "Am Grabe steh'n wir stille"

And a communion hymn that begins *O Liebe, wie gross / Und schon ist das Loos* ("O love, how divine / A blessing is mine").
