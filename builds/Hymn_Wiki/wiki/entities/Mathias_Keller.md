# Mathias Keller

Mathias Keller was a German musician who made his home in Boston and produced the piece of music for which he is principally remembered: a patriotic hymn that stood alongside "The Star-Spangled Banner" and "My Country, 'Tis of Thee" in the patriotic repertoire of the late nineteenth century.

## Life

Keller was born in 1813 and came to America, settling in Boston, where he became part of the city's active musical life. He died in 1875.

## "The American Hymn"

The text known as "The American Hymn," beginning "Speed Our Republic, O Father on High," was set to music by Keller. Metcalf devotes a chapter to both the text and the tune, tracing the history of the piece and its reception in American life. The composition was well-known at public celebrations and patriotic occasions in the post-Civil War decades.

Keller's musical style was rooted in his German training, and the hymn has the broad, solemn quality of a national anthem. It is not as well remembered today as Ward's "Materna" (used for "America the Beautiful") or the tunes of the New England tradition, but it represents the German immigrant contribution to American civic religious culture.

Metcalf's chapter on "The American Hymn" is the final substantial section of his book, indicating the importance he placed on this composition in the story of American sacred music. (Metcalf, pp. 362-366)
