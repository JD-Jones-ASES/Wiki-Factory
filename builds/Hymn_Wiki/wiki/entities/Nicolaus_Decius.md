# Nicolaus Decius

## The Monk Who Gave the Reformation Its Canticles

Nicolaus Decius was a Catholic monk in the cloister of Steterburg who embraced the Lutheran Reformation and brought with him one extraordinary gift: the ability to transform ancient liturgical canticles into magnificent congregational hymns.

## Life

Decius later became pastor of St. Nicholas Church in Stettin, where he died under mysterious circumstances in 1541. Beyond these bare facts, little is known of his life.

## His Two Masterpieces

Luther prized both of Decius's hymns highly and included them in his German liturgy. They were published as early as 1526 in a little hymn collection at Rostock in the Platt-Deutsch dialect.

**"All Glory Be to Thee, Most High"** (*Allein Gott in der Höh sei Ehr*) --- a metrical version of the ancient canticle *Gloria in Excelsis Deo*:
> *All glory be to Thee, Most High,*
> *To Thee all adoration!*
> *In grace and truth Thou drawest nigh*
> *To offer us salvation.*

**"O Lamb of God, Most Holy"** (*O Lamm Gottes unschuldig*) --- a metrical *Agnus Dei*, equally beautiful:
> *O Lamb of God, most holy,*
> *On Calvary an offering;*
> *Despised, meek, and lowly,*
> *Thou in Thy death and suffering*
> *Our sins didst bear, our anguish;*
> *The might of death didst vanquish;*
> *Give us Thy peace, O Jesus!*

Decius was also apparently a musician of some note; the two magnificent chorale melodies to which these hymns are sung are generally credited to him, though some uncertainty surrounds their composition. Whether he composed them or inherited them, he gave the Reformation two of its most serene and glorious canticles.
