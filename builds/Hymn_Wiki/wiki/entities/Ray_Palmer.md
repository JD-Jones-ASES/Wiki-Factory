# Ray Palmer

*"The most precious contribution which American genius has yet made to the hymnology of the Christian Church"* --- that is how Ryden describes "My faith looks up to Thee." It came from the pen of a very humble but gifted Congregational minister named Ray Palmer, who wrote it at the age of twenty-two, with no thought that it would ever become a hymn.

## Descended from the Pilgrims

Palmer was born at Little Compton, Rhode Island, on November 12, 1808. He was a direct descendant of John Alden and his wife Priscilla, those early Pilgrim settlers immortalized by Longfellow. One of his forebears was William Palmer, who came to Plymouth in 1621.

Through the pressure of poverty, Ray found it necessary to leave home at thirteen after a grammar education. For two years he clerked in a Boston dry goods store, during which time he passed through deep spiritual experiences and gave his heart to God. Friends who recognized unusual gifts in the young man urged him to continue his education. Eventually he graduated from Phillips Andover Academy and from Yale.

## The Hymn in the Note-Book

It was while teaching in New York City that "My faith looks up to Thee" was written. He was twenty-two. He tells in his own account how he had been reading a little German poem of two stanzas picturing a penitent sinner before the cross. Deeply moved, he translated them into English and then added the four stanzas that form his own hymn.

*"I gave form to what I felt, by writing, with little effort, the stanzas,"* he said. *"I recollect I wrote them with very tender emotion, and ended the last lines with tears."*

He copied the poem into a little note-book which he constantly carried in his pocket, reading it often as a part of private devotion. It never occurred to him that it might one day be used as a hymn.

## The Street Corner Meeting

One day as Palmer was walking along the busy streets of Boston, he chanced to meet Lowell Mason, the famous musician and composer, who was compiling a hymn-book and asked if Palmer could give him some words for which he could write music. Palmer remembered the poem in his note-book. The two men stepped into a nearby store, a copy was made, and given to Mason.

When they met again a few days later, Mason exclaimed: *"Dr. Palmer, you may live many years and do many good things, but I think you will be best known to posterity as the author of 'My faith looks up to Thee.'"*

Mason wrote the beautiful tune known as "Olivet" for the hymn. Words and music were wedded, and should never be parted.

> My faith looks up to Thee,
> Thou Lamb of Calvary,
>   Saviour divine;
> Now hear me while I pray,
> Take all my guilt away,
> O let me from this day
>   Be wholly Thine.

The last stanza captures the note of utter trust that made the hymn beloved:

> When ends life's transient dream,
> When death's cold, sullen stream
>   Shall o'er me roll,
> Blest Saviour, then, in love,
> Fear and distrust remove;
> O bear me safe above,
>   A ransomed soul.

## "A Ransomed Soul"

*"A ransomed soul!"* Ryden asks. *"Who would not have been moved to deep emotion after having written a poem with such a sublime closing line!"*

This happened in the year 1830. Almost a century later, it is still one of the most beloved hymns in English.

## The Ministry and the Other Hymns

Palmer was ordained to the Congregational ministry in 1835. He served a congregation in Bath, Maine, for fifteen years, and another at Albany, New York, for a similar period. His whole life was characterized by warm, almost passionate, devotion to Christ.

He wrote many other splendid hymns, some of them fine translations from the Latin. His rendering of Bernard of Clairvaux's "O Jesus, Joy of loving hearts" is a gem of wondrous beauty, widely used as a communion hymn.

His own favorite hymn was "Jesus, these eyes have never seen," which many regard as second only to "My faith looks up to Thee." On the day before his death, he was heard repeating its last stanza:

> When death these mortal eyes shall seal,
>   And still this throbbing heart,
> The rending veil shall Thee reveal,
>   All glorious as Thou art.

Palmer refused to accept money for the use of his hymns. He insisted that publishers print them exactly as written, considering the common practice of tampering with texts "immoral."

He died in 1887.

## Legacy

Ray Palmer is the poet of personal devotion in American hymnody. He wrote not for congregations but for the note-book in his pocket, and God took his private prayer and gave it to the world. "My faith looks up to Thee" is American hymnody's finest hour.
