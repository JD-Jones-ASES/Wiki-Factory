# Joseph Scriven

Joseph Scriven wrote "What a Friend We Have in Jesus" not as an exercise in religious poetry but as a private expression of a faith tested by devastating personal loss. The fact that it became one of the most widely sung gospel hymns in history --- strengthening "many a weak and failing soul to 'try again'" --- is the kind of grace the hymn itself describes.

## A Life Consecrated by Grief

Scriven was born in Dublin, Ireland, in 1820, and graduated at Trinity College. He came to Canada at twenty-five. The accidental death by drowning of his intended bride on the eve of their wedding day led him to consecrate his life and fortune to the service of Christ. He spent the rest of his life in acts of quiet charity, giving away his means and labor freely.

He died in Canada on October 10, 1886.

## "What a Friend We Have in Jesus"

The hymn was written to comfort his mother in Ireland, not for publication. For years Scriven did not claim it. When a neighbor found the manuscript and asked if he had written it, he replied, "The Lord and I did it between us." The story is told at length by [[Ira_Sankey]] in his *Story of the Gospel Hymns* (pp. 245-6), from which Butterworth-Brown draw their account.

The hymn's theology is simple and profound: every grief and burden and failure of faith can be brought to Jesus in prayer. The well-known middle stanza --- "Have we trials and temptations? / Is there trouble anywhere? / We should never be discouraged: / Take it to the Lord in prayer" --- has been the specific message that turned despairing people back toward life.

## The Tune

The music was composed by Charles Crozat Converse, LL.D. (born Warren, Massachusetts, 1832), a musician, lawyer, and writer. Converse had studied in Leipzig and Berlin, written concert overtures and symphonies, and composed numerous sacred and secular pieces. His tune for "What a Friend We Have in Jesus" is the one that carried the hymn to the world.
