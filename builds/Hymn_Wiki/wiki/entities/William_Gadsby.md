# William Gadsby

William Gadsby was born at Attleborough, Warwickshire, about January 3, 1773. His parents were poor, and at thirteen he was apprenticed to a ribbon weaver.

## Conversion and Ministry

The turning point in Gadsby's life came in 1790 when he witnessed an execution. The sight impressed him deeply, and he abandoned his previous course of life. After passing through "various experiences" (Burrage's phrase, suggesting a complex spiritual journey), he was baptized in 1793 and united with the Baptist church at Coventry.

In 1798 he commenced preaching, and two years later a chapel was built for him at Hinckley. In 1805 he accepted the pastorate of the Baptist church in St. George's Road (later called Rochdale Road) in Manchester, where he ministered until his death, January 27, 1844. Burrage notes that he is said to have preached twelve thousand sermons — a number that testifies to extraordinary energy and devotion over a ministry of nearly forty years.

## Hymn Collections

In 1814, Gadsby published his *Nazarene Songs* and also his *Selection of Hymns*. A new edition of the latter appeared in 1838, with a supplement. In 1846–47, all of Hart's hymns not included in earlier editions were added; in 1849–50, a second supplement by Mr. Philpot was added, increasing the whole number to more than eleven hundred. He also published a *Selection of Hymns for Sunday Schools*.

His son John Gadsby was the author of *Memoirs of the Principal Hymn Writers and Compilers of the 17th and 18th Centuries* (2nd ed., London, 1855) — a work that Burrage himself used as a source.

## His Hymns

Among the hymns in his *Nazarene Songs* that appear in various collections is:

*Once more, dear God of grace, / Thine earthly courts we tread; / We come to see thy face, / And banquet with our head.*

Four of Gadsby's hymns, including this one, appear in the appendix to Jones and Allison's *Selection of Psalms and Hymns* (4th ed., Philadelphia, 1819) — indicating his work reached American Baptist circles even in his lifetime.

## Baptist Tradition Context (Burrage)

Gadsby represents the Particular Baptist (Calvinist) wing of English Baptist hymnody — the tradition of strong doctrinal conviction married to warm devotional verse. His *Selection* was the standard hymnal for this stream of Baptist life in England for much of the nineteenth century, much as [[John_Rippon]]'s *Selection* had served an earlier generation.
