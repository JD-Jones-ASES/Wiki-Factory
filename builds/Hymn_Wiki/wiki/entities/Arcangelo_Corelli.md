# Arcangelo Corelli

Arcangelo Corelli was born at Fusignano, near Bologna, Italy, in 1653. He became one of the foremost violinists of his age and a composer whose work shaped the development of the concerto grosso. He died in Rome in 1713.

## His Hymn Tune

Corelli's musical legacy touched congregational song through the tune "Ain," which was set to [[Isaac_Watts]]'s hymn "Come We That Love the Lord." The tune's stately elegance carries something of the same quality Butterworth-Brown ascribed to Corelli as a musician: extraordinary refinement and sensitivity.

## Musical Temperament

Butterworth-Brown record a poignant vignette of Corelli's character. When playing in a concert with [[George_Frederic_Handel]] — the account places this in Naples — he was "not sure of himself in playing French scores" and made a mortifying public mistake. Unlike more confident temperaments, Corelli took the humiliation so deeply to heart that he brooded over it until his death. The same passionate responsiveness that made him a great musician also made him vulnerable to an artist's most painful experience: public failure before a peer.

It is one of the small ironies of hymn history that this brooding perfectionist contributed a tune now sung with joy by ordinary congregations in chapels and churches around the world.
