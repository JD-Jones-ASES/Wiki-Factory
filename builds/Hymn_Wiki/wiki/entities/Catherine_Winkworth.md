# Catherine Winkworth

Catherine Winkworth was born in London on September 13, 1827. She became one of the most industrious and successful translators of German hymns into English, contributing the results of her work to two English editions of *Lyra Germanica*, to the *Church Book of England*, and to *Christian Singers of Germany*. She died in 1878.

Her work opened the treasury of German Lutheran hymnody to English-speaking congregations at a time when that tradition was little known in Britain. Through her translations, the hymns of [[Paul_Gerhardt]], Luther, and dozens of other German hymn-writers entered the English hymnal.

## Notable Translations

Among Winkworth's many translations is the English version of "Fear Not, O Little Flock" — the battle hymn composed by the Rev. Johan Michael Altenburg on receiving news of Gustavus Adolphus's victory at Leipzig in 1631. The hymn was sung by the Swedish and Protestant armies before the battle of Lützen, where the king was killed at the moment of victory. Winkworth's translation preserves the martial confidence of the original:

> Fear not, O little flock, the foe
> Who madly seeks your overthrow,
>   Dread not his rage and power.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Butterworth-Brown credit Winkworth's translations with the care and faithfulness that distinguished the best Victorian philological scholarship. Her work was systematic — not merely occasional poetry but a deliberate project of cultural transfer. The German chorale tradition, with its thick harmonies and sturdy stanzas, needed an interpreter who respected both the original and the capabilities of the receiving tradition. Winkworth was that interpreter.
