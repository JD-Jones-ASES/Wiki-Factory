# Timothy Dwight

Timothy Dwight was born at Northampton, Massachusetts, on May 14, 1752, and graduated at Yale College at the age of thirteen. He wrote several religious poems of considerable length. In 1795 he was elected President of Yale College, and in 1800 he revised Watts's Psalms at the request of the General Association of Connecticut, adding a number of translations of his own. He died on January 11, 1817.

## I Love Thy Kingdom, Lord

Butterworth-Brown describe "I Love Thy Kingdom, Lord" — known as "Dr. Dwight's Hymn" *par eminence* — as "one of the imperishable lyrics of the Christian Church." They write that "the real spirit of the 122nd Psalm is in it, and it is worthy of Watts in his best moments":

> I love Thy kingdom, Lord,
>   The house of Thine abode,
> The Church our blest Redeemer saved
>   With His own precious blood.

The tune "St. Thomas" by Aaron Williams (1734-1776) is most commonly associated with the hymn. (Hubert P. Main believed the author to be Handel, since it appeared in Williams's 1762 collection containing pieces by the great masters — though it was neither credited to Handel nor claimed by Williams himself.)

## Musical and Thematic Context

Butterworth-Brown recall Dwight's ardent devotion to [[Oliver_Holden]]'s tune "Coronation" — which he loved so much that the Yale College choir "often sang it while he, catching, as it were, the music of the heavenly world, would join them, and lead with the most ardent devotion." This image of the president of Yale caught up in the rapture of a carpenter's tune is one of the memorable vignettes of American hymn history.

His revision of Watts's Psalms was a significant contribution to American congregational song, as it adapted the English Dissenter's masterwork to the idiom and needs of the new republic's churches.
