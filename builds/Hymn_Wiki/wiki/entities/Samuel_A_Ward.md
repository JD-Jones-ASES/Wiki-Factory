# Samuel Augustus Ward

Samuel Augustus Ward composed many tunes, but he is remembered for one --- and it became one of the most recognized melodies in American life.

## Life

Ward was born in Newark, New Jersey, on December 28, 1848. For more than twenty-five years he ran a music store in his native city, selling pianos and sheet music. He was the leader of the Orpheus Club of Newark for fourteen years, a familiar figure in the city's musical community. He resigned as conductor of the club at the close of the 1902-03 season and died in Newark on September 28, 1903.

## "Materna" and "America the Beautiful"

Ward composed a tune that appeared in hymnals under several names --- "Caldwell," "Resurrection," and most commonly "Materna." In the hymnals of his own day, the tune was set to the old hymn "O Mother, Dear Jerusalem."

But during the First World War, the tune became permanently paired with Katharine Lee Bates's poem "America the Beautiful" ("O beautiful for spacious skies"). As Metcalf wrote in 1925: "During the Great War, however, it became associated with Miss Bates' patriotic hymn, 'America, the Beautiful,' and the more recent books use this tune and hymn together." (Metcalf, p. 307-308)

Ward did not live to see the full flowering of this association, but his tune proved perfectly matched to words of national aspiration --- spacious, dignified, and memorable.
