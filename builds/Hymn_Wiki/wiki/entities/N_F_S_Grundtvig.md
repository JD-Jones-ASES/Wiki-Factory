# N.F.S. Grundtvig

Nikolai F. S. Grundtvig was the last and greatest of the celebrated triumvirate of Danish hymn-writers. As [[Thomas_Kingo]] was the bright star of the seventeenth century and [[Hans_Adolph_Brorson]] of the eighteenth, so Grundtvig shone with a luster all his own in the nineteenth. The "poet of Easter" and the "poet of Christmas" were succeeded by the "poet of Whitsuntide."

## The Poet of Whitsuntide

The appellation was not without reason. It was Grundtvig, above all others, who strove mightily in Denmark against the deadening spirit of rationalism that had dried up the streams of spirituality in the Church. Rationalism had worked havoc with the most sacred truths of Christianity. It had, as someone put it, *"converted the banner of the Lamb into a blue-striped handkerchief, the Christian religion into a philosophy of happiness, and the temple dome into a parasol."*

Under its influence, ministers had reduced worship to lectures on science and domestic economy. One preacher reportedly developed the theme of Christ and the manger into a lecture on the proper care of stables. Another, moved by the story of the holy women coming to the sepulcher on Easter morning, delivered a peroration on the advantages of getting up early. God was referred to as "Providence," Christ as "the founder of Christianity," sin as "error," and salvation as "happiness."

Grundtvig fought all this with the passion of a man who felt Pentecost itself was at stake.

## Early Life and Lost Faith

Grundtvig was born in Udby, Denmark, on September 8, 1783. His father was one of the few Lutheran pastors in Denmark who had remained faithful to evangelical truth. But young Grundtvig, studying at university, came under the influence of the "new theology" and finished his academic career "without spirit and without faith."

A series of shocks opened his eyes. In 1807, he saw the population of Copenhagen laughing and dancing while the Danish fleet was being destroyed by English warships and the capital itself was being bombarded. The frivolity of a spiritually empty people struck him with the force of a revelation.

In 1810, he preached his famous probation sermon on the theme: *"Why has the Word of God departed from His house?"* The sermon produced a sensation. From this time Grundtvig was known as a mystic and a fanatic. His career was checkered, but throughout his life he exerted powerful influence by his writing as well as by his preaching. His poetry and hymns attracted so much attention that it was said "Kingo's harp has been strung afresh."

## The Hymns

Grundtvig's strongest hymns are those that deal with the Church and the sacraments. He taught that the divine character of the Church was real --- that Christ not only founded it, but as the Living Word, He is present in it and in the sacraments unto the end of time.

*"Built on the Rock, the Church doth stand"* is probably his most famous hymn:

> Built on the Rock the Church doth stand,
>   Even when steeples are falling;
> Crumbled have spires in every land,
>   Bells still are chiming and calling;
> Calling the young and old to rest,
> But above all the soul distressed,
>   Longing for rest everlasting.

He could also write with charming freshness, as in his Christmas hymn:

> Chime, happy Christmas bells, once more!
> The heavenly Guest is at the door...

The Danish hymnologist Brandt observed that Grundtvig's characteristic symbol was the bird. His hymns are primarily hymns of the Spirit --- lauding the Holy Spirit, the Giver and Renewer of life, who bears us up on mighty wings toward the mansions of light.

Other noted hymns by Grundtvig include "Love, the fount of light from heaven," "A Babe is born in Bethlehem," "O let Thy Spirit with us tarry," "The peace of God protects our hearts," and "He who has helped me hitherto."

## Father of the Public High School

Because of his intensive efforts to reform education in his day, Grundtvig became known as "the father of the public high school in Scandinavia." His vision for an education rooted in living speech, folk tradition, and communal life rather than abstract scholarship shaped Scandinavian education for generations.

## The Long Life

In 1861, when he celebrated his golden jubilee as pastor, Grundtvig was given the title of bishop. He was already seventy-eight years old. He lived to eighty-nine --- preaching his last sermon on the day before his death, on September 2, 1872. He was made bishop, in the end, only because the Danish Church recognized there was no appropriate category for what he was.

## Legacy

Grundtvig stands as the greatest figure in Scandinavian hymnody. The three Danish hymnists between them --- Kingo's sun, Brorson's rose, and Grundtvig's bird --- represent a full theology of Christ: Redeemer, intimate Friend, and Living Spirit. Together they gave the Danish church a singing voice of extraordinary richness.
