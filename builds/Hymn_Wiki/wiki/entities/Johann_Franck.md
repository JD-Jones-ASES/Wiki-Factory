# Johann Franck

## The Layman Who Wrote for the Altar

Johann Franck stands as an example of the extraordinary breadth of Lutheran hymnody in seventeenth-century Germany: he was not a pastor or theologian but a lawyer, later mayor of his native town of Guben, Brandenburg, who in the midst of civic and legal duties produced hymns of enduring beauty.

## Life

Born in 1618, Franck lived through the [[Thirty_Years_War_Hymns|Thirty Years' War]] and its aftermath. The depth of suffering and the intense spiritual life of the Lutheran laity in that era are reflected in his hymns. He became mayor of Guben in 1661 and died in 1677.

## The Finest Communion Hymn

Franck is best known for **"Deck Thyself, My Soul, with Gladness"** (*Schmücke dich, O liebe Seele*), which Ryden calls "the finest communion hymn in the German language." It invites the soul to come to the Lord's Table with joy and wonder, dressed not in outward finery but in the adornment of faith and love. Johann Sebastian Bach was deeply moved by this hymn and set it in one of his most tender organ preludes.

## The "Jesus Hymns"

Franck is credited with beginning the long tradition of so-called "Jesus hymns" in German hymnody --- hymns in which the soul addresses Christ directly with intimate love and longing, hymns that begin "Jesus, ..." or pivot on the beloved name. This tradition reached its fullest development later in the [[Pietism|Pietist school]], but Franck planted the seed.

His beautiful **"Jesus, Priceless Treasure"** (*Jesu, meine Freude*) is perhaps the finest of all German Jesus hymns. Bach set it as a motet for double chorus that stands among his greatest choral works.

Other hymns by Franck still in use include:
- **"Light of the Gentile Nations"**
- **"Lord, to Thee I Make Confession"**
- **"Lord God, We Worship Thee"**

Franck articulated his view of the purpose of poetry: "the nurse of piety, the herald of immortality, the promoter of cheerfulness, the conqueror of sadness, and a foretaste of heavenly glory." His hymns embody every one of those aims.
