# William G. Fischer

William G. Fischer was born in Baltimore, October 14, 1835. At the age of eight he was chosen to lead the singing in a Baltimore church. For the ten years from 1858 to 1868 he was professor of music at Girard College in Philadelphia. He was a teacher of harmony and piano for many years, and also a dealer in musical instruments. He died August 12, 1912, at the age of seventy.

## "I Love to Tell the Story"

Fischer composed the tune for Katherine Hankey's hymn "I Love to Tell the Story," said to have been written expressly for Bishop Charles C. McCabe. This is his best-known surviving composition.

He also composed the tune for William McDonald's hymn "I Am Coming to the Cross" (first line), which appeared in *The Revivalist* and has been in common use since that date. Other tunes include "Whiter Than Snow" (which enjoyed long popularity), "I Am Trusting, Lord, in Thee," and "A Little Talk with Jesus." Over two hundred tunes came from his pen.

## Chorus Leadership

Fischer was exceptionally successful in directing large choruses. During the Moody and Sankey meetings in Philadelphia he directed a choir of one thousand persons. At the bicentennial of the landing of William Penn he led a large chorus of Welsh voices.

Though active in the religious work of the Christ Methodist Episcopal Church in Philadelphia during his residence there, he refused to hold any office. (Metcalf, p. 322-323)
