# Hugh Stowell

The Rev. Hugh Stowell was born at Douglas on the Isle of Man on December 3, 1799. Educated at Oxford and ordained to the ministry in 1823, he received twelve years later the appointment of Canon to Chester Cathedral. He was a popular and effective preacher and a graceful writer. Forty-seven hymns are credited to him. He died in 1865.

## From Every Stormy Wind That Blows

This hymn of Christian peace has carried consolation to souls in both ordinary and extraordinary trials for nearly two centuries. Its most memorable use was its darkest: it was sung by eight American missionaries — the Revs. Albert Johnson, John E. Freeman, David E. Campbell and their wives, and Mr. and Mrs. McMullen, and two small children, Fannie and Willie Campbell — when they were taken prisoner and put to death at Cawnpore by order of the bloody Nana Sahib in 1857.

> From every stormy wind that blows,
> From every swelling tide of woes
> There is a calm, a sure retreat;
> 'Tis found beneath the Mercy Seat.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Butterworth-Brown note that the hymn "has practically but one tune — it has been sung to Hastings' 'Retreat' ever since the music was made." The tune "Retreat" by [[Thomas_Hastings]] — with its quality of stillness — is perfectly suited to the hymn's imagery of sanctuary beneath the mercy seat.

Stowell is one of those figures who wrote generously and well, and whose reputation rests on a single hymn: one that found its ultimate use not in peaceful chapels but in a prisoner's last hour.
