# S. Dryden Phelps

The Rev. Sylvester Dryden Phelps, D.D., was born in Suffield, Connecticut, on May 15, 1816. He studied at the Connecticut Literary Institution and, in an early call to the ministry, gave his talents to the service of the church. His long settlement — comprising what Butterworth-Brown call "his principal life work" — was in New Haven, where he served as pastor of the First Baptist Church for twenty-nine years. He died there on November 23, 1895.

## Saviour, Thy Dying Love

Phelps wrote "Saviour, Thy Dying Love" in 1862. It was first published in *Pure Gold* (1871) and afterwards in the Baptist Hymn and Tune Book. The Rev. [[Robert_Lowry]] admired the hymn and composed a tune for it "perfectly suited to its metre and spirit." The hymn was inserted by Ira Sankey in *Gospel Hymns No. 1* and since won its way into several revival collections and nondenominational manuals.

> Saviour, Thy dying love
>   Thou gavest me,
> Nor should I aught withhold
>   Dear Lord, from Thee.
>
> Give me a faithful heart,
>   Likeness to Thee,
> That each departing day
>   Henceforth may see
> Some work of love begun,
> Some deed of kindness done,
> Some wand'rer sought and won,
>   Something for Thee.

The penultimate line, originally "Some sinful wanderer won," was altered by the author himself. The usual title of Lowry's tune is "Something for Jesus."

## Baptist Tradition Context (Burrage)

Burrage traces Phelps's career in full. After his conversion at eighteen, he joined the Second Baptist Church in Suffield, Connecticut, while a student at the Connecticut Literary Institution. He entered Brown University in 1840 and graduated in 1844. After studying at Yale Theological Seminary he supplied the Baptist church in Bristol and later the First Baptist Church in New Haven, where he became pastor on January 21, 1846. In twenty-eight years of ministry there, 1,217 people united with the church — 615 by baptism. Madison University conferred the D.D. in 1854.

In 1874 Phelps moved to the Jefferson Street Baptist Church in Providence; in 1876 he became editor and proprietor of the Christian Secretary in Hartford, a prominent Baptist paper which he edited for twelve years. He was elected a trustee of Brown University in 1879.

Burrage documents a longer hymn-writing history than is usually noted. Phelps's hymns began in his college years — he left Brown temporarily in 1841 to teach in Suffield, and while there wrote two hymns for a juvenile temperance celebration, including "Father, from thy throne above," which found its way into Methodist Episcopal hymnals. Over the following years he contributed to the Plymouth Collection edition for Baptist churches (1857), the Baptist Praise Book (1872), the Baptist Hymn and Tune Book (1873), and the Service of Song (1871). He also published several books including "Holy Land: with Glimpses of Europe and Egypt" (1862), which went through nine editions.
