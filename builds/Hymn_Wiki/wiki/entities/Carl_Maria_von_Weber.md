# Carl Maria von Weber

Carl Maria von Weber was born in Eutin, Germany, in 1786, the son of a roving musician. He developed no remarkable genius until he was about twenty years old — though as a fine vocalist, his singing brought him popularity and gain. In 1806 he nearly lost his voice by accidentally drinking nitric acid, a calamity from which he partially but never fully recovered. He served for several years as private secretary to Duke Ludwig at Stuttgart, then as Chapel-Master at Prague (1813), and from there went to Dresden in 1817 as Music-Director.

Weber's *Körner* songs won the hearts of all Germany, and his immortal opera *Der Freischütz* (The Free Archer) and numerous tender melodies spread his reputation to all civilized nations. Butterworth-Brown describe him simply: "No other composer had such feeling for beauty of sound." He was physically frail and delicate, and died of untimely decline during a visit to London in 1826 — aged forty.

## The Tune "Wilmot"

Weber's tune "Wilmot" became the standard setting for [[Joseph_Hart]]'s "Come Ye Sinners Poor and Needy." Butterworth-Brown note that the hymn "is now more generally sung" to "Wilmot" — dropping the echo lines at the end of each stanza. The tune's warmth and supple movement suited Hart's urgent invitation perfectly.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Weber is one of several great secular composers whose music found its way into the hymnal — not through any deliberate act of piety on the composer's part, but because the church recognized something in the music that served its devotional purposes. In this he joins Rousseau ("Greenville"), Haydn ("Haydn"), and others whose melodies were too good to leave in the concert hall. Weber himself, whatever his personal faith, left the church one of the most beautiful tunes in its invitation repertoire.
