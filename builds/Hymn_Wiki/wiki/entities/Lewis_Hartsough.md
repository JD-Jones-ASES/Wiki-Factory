# Lewis Hartsough

Lewis Hartsough was born in Ithaca, New York, August 31, 1828. He joined the Oneida Conference of the Methodist Episcopal Church in New York in 1851. After holding several appointments, his health failed and he was sent to the Rocky Mountain district, where he organized the Utah Mission and became its first superintendent. He continued to live in the West, residing at Mount Vernon, Iowa, when he died January 1, 1919. Earlier hymnals had listed him as "died in 1870" — a premature report that amused him into old age.

## The Revivalist

Hartsough was the musical editor of *The Revivalist*, compiled by Joseph Hillman (1833-1890) of Troy, New York. He encountered Hillman when the latter came to conduct revival meetings in the South Street Methodist Church in Utica, where Hartsough was then the pastor. The book's first copyright was entered February 10, 1868, and successive editions eventually reached 336 pages. Combined circulation exceeded 150,000 copies.

In one edition of *The Revivalist*, Hartsough contributed twelve hymns (words), fourteen of his own tunes, and thirty arrangements. Bishop McCabe considered his best hymn to be "Let Me Go" (written in 1862). Ira D. Sankey considered "I Hear Thy Welcome Voice" (1872) — also known as "I Am Coming, Lord" — his best. Hartsough noted with satisfaction that "last month a publisher, asking for it, said 'I Am Coming, Lord' is often sung in the trenches of war-cursed Europe." (Written in September 1916, after nearly ninety years of life.) (Metcalf, p. 312-316)

Of all Hartsough's tunes, only "I Am Coming, Lord" survived into hymnals of the present day.
