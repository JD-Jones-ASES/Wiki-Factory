# William Rees (Gwilym Hiraethog)

William Rees, known by his bardic name Gwilym Hiraethog (William of the Moorlands), wrote what may be the most beloved Welsh hymn of the nineteenth century --- a poem whose Welsh original carries an emotional weight that translations have struggled to fully render.

## Life

Rees was born in 1802 and died in 1883. He was a Congregationalist minister and a significant figure in Welsh literary and religious culture, known both as a preacher and as a man of letters. "Hiraethog" refers to the moorland district of northeast Wales where he was formed, and the word *hiraeth* --- meaning longing, homesickness, grief for what is lost or distant --- runs through Welsh consciousness and Welsh hymnody alike.

## "Dyma Gariad Fel y Moroedd"

The hymn's title translates roughly as "Here Is Love Vast as the Ocean" or, in Butterworth-Brown's rendering, "Love Unfathomed as the Ocean." It meditates on the love of God as an ocean without shore or bottom --- the great Welsh image of boundlessness applied to divine grace. The hymn became particularly associated with the Welsh Revival of 1904-5, when Evan Roberts used it as one of the central songs of the meetings, and vast crowds sang it in the distinctive Welsh manner --- in parts, from memory, with mounting intensity.

The hymn is sung at Welsh funerals around the world, especially among the diaspora communities of Pennsylvania, Ohio, Patagonia, and beyond. To hear it sung by a Welsh congregation is to hear the tradition at its most characteristic: the theology of grace expressed in music that does not separate the intellectual from the emotional.
