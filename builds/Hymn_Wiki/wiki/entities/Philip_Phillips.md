# Philip Phillips

Philip Phillips was one of the great evangelistic singers of the nineteenth century --- a man whose gift for sacred song planted seeds that grew into movements. He was known as "The Singing Pilgrim," and the title suited him: his whole life was a pilgrimage of song.

## His Influence on Ira Sankey

When Ira David Sankey was a young man, he heard Philip Phillips sing in public. The impression was decisive. Butterworth-Brown write that Sankey "heard Philip Phillips sing impressed him deeply... with the power of a gifted solo vocalist over assembled multitudes." This encounter helped awaken Sankey to his own calling as a gospel singer --- making Phillips, in a real sense, the indirect inspiration for the entire Moody-Sankey revival movement.

## The Singing Pilgrim

Phillips's title "The Singing Pilgrim" came from his published songbook of the same name, released in 1865. The compilation was built around the imagery of Bunyan's *Pilgrim's Progress* --- Christian and Hopeful making their way toward the Celestial City. It was for this collection that Phillips wrote to Mrs. Ellen M.H. Gates in 1865, asking her to write a hymn inspired by Bunyan's description of the joyful music of heaven when Christian and Hopeful enter on its shore beyond the river of death.

## "Home of the Soul"

Mrs. Gates complied, and Phillips received her poem, sat down with his little boy on his knee, read again the Bunyan passage, then turned to his organ, pencil in hand, and pricked out the notes of the melody. The result was "I Will Sing You a Song of That Beautiful Land" --- known as "Home of the Soul."

In Phillips's own words about the hymn: "It has been a comfort to many a bereaved soul. Like many loved hymns, it has had a peculiar history, for its simple melody has flowed from the lips of High Churchmen, and has sought to make itself heard above the din of Salvation Army cymbals and drums. It has been sung in prisons and in jailyards, while the poor convict was waiting to be launched into eternity, and on hundreds of funeral occasions. One man writes me that he has led the singing of it at one hundred and twenty funerals. It was sung at my dear boy's funeral, who sat on my knee when I wrote it. I have books containing the song now printed in seven different languages."

## The Evangelistic Hymns

During the Moody-Sankey missions in England and Scotland (1873), Moody and Sankey used Phillips's compilation *Hallowed Songs* as their hymn-book before Sankey began publishing his own pieces. This book, with some of Phillips's own hymns included, was the foundation on which the *Gospel Hymns* series was eventually built.

Phillips represents the generation that bridged the old revival tradition of camp-meeting songs and Elder Swan with the polished evangelical movement of Moody and Sankey. His singing was a link in the chain.

## Legacy

Butterworth-Brown place Philip Phillips in the succession of home-grown American gospel singers that includes the Hutchinson family and Ossian Dodge. These were men and women who carried the gospel in music before the great evangelistic organizations existed, singing wherever people would listen. His influence on Sankey alone would earn him a permanent place in the history of revival hymnody.
