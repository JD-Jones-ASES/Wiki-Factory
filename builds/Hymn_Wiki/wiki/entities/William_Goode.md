# William Goode

William Goode was born in Buckingham, England, on April 2, 1762. He studied for the ministry and became a curate, the successor of William Romaine. He helped found the English Church Missionary Society and served for twenty years as Secretary of the "Society for the Relief of Poor Pious Clergymen."

His spiritual maturity came early, and his habits of thought were formed amid associations like those the young Wesleys and Whitefield sought. Even in his student days he proved his aspiration for purer religious life by an evangelical zeal that cost him the ridicule of many school-fellows — but the meetings for conference and prayer which he organized among them were not unattended, and were lasting and salutary in their effect.

Jesus was the theme of his life and song, and was his last word. He died in 1816.

## Crown His Head with Endless Blessing

Butterworth-Brown write that for celebrating the praise of the Saviour, Goode "seems to have been of like spirit and genius with Perronet" — the author of "All Hail the Power of Jesus' Name." His hymn shares that hymn's crown imagery and adoring intent:

> Crown His head with endless blessing
>   Who in God the Father's name
> With compassion never ceasing
>   Comes salvation to proclaim.
> Hail, ye saints who know His favor,
>   Who within His gates are found.
> Hail, ye saints, th' exalted Saviour,
>   Let His courts with praise resound.

The *Methodist Hymnal* includes one of his doxologies in its collection of ten.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Haydn's tune "Haydn" is used in several important hymnals as the chosen music for Goode's praise hymn. Butterworth-Brown write that "its strain and spirit are lofty and melodious and in entire accord with the pious poet's praise." The natural fitness of a Haydn tune — with its characteristic nobility and warmth — for Goode's words is one of those happy unions that sustains both text and melody through generations of use.
