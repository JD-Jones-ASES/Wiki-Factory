# Benjamin Schmolck

## Gerhardt's Heir in the 18th Century

Of all the German hymn-writers in the early eighteenth century, Benjamin Schmolck is the one whose name appears most frequently in modern hymnals --- second only to [[Paul_Gerhardt]] himself. His 1,183 hymns blend the objective Lutheran faith of the Reformation with the personal warmth of the [[Pietism|Pietist movement]], without surrendering to the emotional excesses of the Halle school.

## Biographical Sketch

Schmolck was born at Brauchitzdorf, Silesia, in 1672, the son of a pastor. His path to the ministry included a remarkable moment: when he came home from school and was invited to preach in his father's pulpit, the sermon so impressed his father that he immediately sent the young man to the University of Leipzig for formal theological training.

In 1702 Schmolck became pastor of the Friedenskirche in Schweidnitz, Silesia, where he served for thirty-five years. The circumstances were difficult: under the terms of the Peace of Westphalia (1648), all proper church buildings in that district had been turned over to the Catholics. The Lutherans were permitted only a "meeting-house" of timber and clay --- no tower, no bells. Even administering communion to the dying required Catholic permission. Schmolck labored patiently in this constrained situation, and his hymns became famous throughout all Germany.

## His Hymns

Among the best known of his 1,183 hymns:

**"My Jesus, as Thou Wilt"** (*Mein Jesu wie du willst*) --- a hymn of surrender and trust, praying that Christ's will might govern every circumstance:
> *My Jesus, as Thou wilt!*
> *O may Thy will be mine!*
> *Into Thy hand of love*
> *I would my all resign.*

**"Light of Light, Enlighten Me"** (*Licht vom Licht, erleuchte mich*) --- a noble Sunday morning hymn. Bach wedded it to a glorious chorale.

**"Open Now Thy Gates of Beauty"** (*Thuet mir auf die schoene Pforte*) --- a hymn for entering the house of worship:
> *Open now thy gates of beauty,*
> *Zion, let me enter there,*
> *Where my soul in joyful duty*
> *Waits for Him who answers prayer.*

Other well-known hymns include **"Welcome, Thou Victor in the Strife"**, **"Blessed Jesus, Here We Stand"**, **"What Our Father Does Is Well"**, **"My God, I Know That I Must Die"**, and **"Precious Word from God in Heaven."**

## Connection to Bach

Schmolck and his contemporary Erdmann Neumeister were the chief Lutheran hymnists of the age when Johann Sebastian Bach was active in Leipzig and Hamburg. Bach set several of their hymns, helping to make these texts immortal in a new form. Schmolck's "Light of Light, Enlighten Me" was one that Bach treated with particular beauty.

## Significance

Schmolck represents the best of conservative Lutheran piety in the eighteenth century --- a man who maintained doctrinal fidelity without stiffness, who could write with warmth and simplicity without lapsing into the sentimentality that would characterize lesser Pietist hymn-writers. His enormous output (over a thousand hymns from a man working under significant ecclesiastical constraints) testifies to a spirit that could not be silenced.
