# Joseph Anstice

Joseph Anstice (1808–1836) was an English classical scholar and hymn writer whose fifty-two hymns were composed during the last evenings of his brief life.

## Life and Career

Anstice was born in Shropshire, England, in 1808. Soon after leaving Oxford University, where he took a high stand as a student, he became Professor of Classical Literature in King's College, London. He was a member of the Church of England.

He died on February 29, 1836, being only twenty-eight years old.

## His Hymns

It was during the last evenings of his life, when he was a great sufferer, that Anstice dictated to his wife the hymns — fifty-two in number — which were collected and published the year he died, for private distribution. From this collection the hymn beginning "O Lord, how happy should we be" was taken.

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

- "O Lord, how happy should we be"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett present Anstice as a figure whose brief, suffering-shaped contribution to hymnody has a special poignancy. The circumstances of composition — a young scholar dictating to his wife as he lay dying — give the hymns a quality of hard-won Christian serenity. The collection was published for private distribution, but the hymns found their way into wider use through their spiritual quality. Nutter's source refers to this collection as "Hymns by the Late Joseph Anstice, M.A."
