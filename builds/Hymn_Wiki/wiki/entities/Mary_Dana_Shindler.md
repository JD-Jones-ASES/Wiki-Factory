# Mary Stanley Bunce Dana Shindler

Mary Stanley Bunce Dana — who later became Mrs. Shindler — wrote from within the Southern evangelical tradition of the mid-nineteenth century, producing hymns of pilgrimage and consolation that carried the tone of a faith tested by transience.

## Life

She was born in Beaufort, South Carolina, in 1810, and died in Texas in 1883. The arc of her life from the Carolina coast to the Texas frontier mirrors something of the pilgrim theme in her most famous hymn.

## "I'm a Pilgrim and I'm a Stranger"

This hymn gives direct voice to the New Testament image of the Christian as a sojourner on earth, seeking a permanent home. Its tone is not mournful but forward-looking: the pilgrim knows where the journey is going and sings about the destination. The hymn has continued in use in traditions that emphasize the other-worldly orientation of Christian faith.

## Place in the Tradition

Butterworth-Brown situate her among the writers of consolatory and hope-oriented hymns that characterized American evangelical writing between the revivals. Her work represents the quieter register of that tradition --- not the urgent call of the mourner's bench but the steady confidence of a faith that has already made its peace with mortality.
