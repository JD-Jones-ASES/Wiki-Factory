# John Pierpont

John Pierpont was one of the more combative figures in nineteenth-century American religious life --- a Unitarian minister, an abolitionist, a temperance advocate, and a poet who believed verse could serve civic virtue as readily as personal piety.

## Life

Pierpont was born in Litchfield, Connecticut, on April 6, 1785. He pursued law, business, and eventually divinity, settling as minister of Hollis Street Church in Boston. His outspoken advocacy for abolition and temperance brought him into repeated conflict with his congregation, a tension that eventually led to his departure from that pulpit. He died in Medford, Massachusetts, on August 26, 1866, at the age of eighty-one.

## "Warren's Address at the Battle of Bunker Hill"

This poem presents an imagined speech by General Joseph Warren to his troops before the Battle of Bunker Hill in 1775 --- Warren was killed in that battle, one of the first American officers to die in the Revolutionary War. The poem captures the spirit of republican sacrifice: the willingness to die for liberty, the acceptance of death as a soldier's honor. It became one of the standard patriotic recitation pieces of the nineteenth century and found its way into readers, schoolbooks, and occasional hymn settings.

## Place in the Tradition

Butterworth-Brown include Pierpont in their account of patriotic hymnody because his work sits at the intersection of civic verse and sacred occasion. His poetry was not primarily hymnodic, but in the tradition of patriotic singing it occupied an honored place alongside more conventionally religious texts.
