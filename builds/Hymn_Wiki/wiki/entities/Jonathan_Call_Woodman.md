# Jonathan Call Woodman

Jonathan Call Woodman was a Boston musician whose career intertwined with those of Lowell Mason, George F. Root, and other leading figures of mid-nineteenth-century American sacred music.

## Life

Woodman was born in 1813 and spent much of his career in Boston as an organist and music teacher. He was connected to the network of educators around Lowell Mason and taught in the Boston public schools. His sister, Mary Olive Woodman, married George Frederick Root in August 1845 --- a personal bond that deepened his professional connections.

Woodman lived to the age of eighty-one, dying in 1894.

## "State Street"

Woodman's most enduring composition is the hymn tune "State Street," a dignified melody that appeared in several mid-century collections and was known to Root and others in the Boston musical circle. Root mentions meeting the composer of "State Street" when they were both teaching in the public schools of Boston. (Metcalf, p. 290)

Like many of his contemporaries, Woodman contributed to the great project of providing American churches with a body of original, singable, and spiritually suitable tunes --- a project that transformed Protestant worship in the nineteenth century.
