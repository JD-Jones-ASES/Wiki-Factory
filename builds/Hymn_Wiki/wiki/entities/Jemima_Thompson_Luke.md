# Jemima Thompson Luke

Jemima Thompson Luke wrote one hymn that became the world's. "I Think When I Read That Sweet Story of Old" --- the hymn of a child who wishes she had been there when Jesus called the little ones to himself --- came to her not at a desk, but in motion: in a stage-coach returning from a visit to a mission school for poor children.

## The Hymn's Origin

The words came to her on the road, inspired by a visit to a mission school. The hymn speaks its own purpose plainly: to awaken religious feeling in young hearts and sanctify the natural childlike interest in Jesus calling the children to himself. Butterworth-Brown describe it honestly: "This is not poetry, but it phrases a wish in a child's own way, to be melodized and fixed in a child's reverent and sensitive memory." They meant it as a compliment: the hymn does exactly what it intends to do.

Mrs. Luke was born at Colebrook Terrace, near London, on August 19, 1813. She was an accomplished and benevolent lady who devoted herself to the education and welfare of the poor. Her hymn was first sung in a village school at Poundford Park and was not published until 1841.

## The Hymn's Musical Life

A remarkable testimony to the hymn's vitality is how many composers have set it to music, and how many hymnbook editors have kept it, printing it to different harmonies across successive editions. The long, spondaic lines are musically compulsory --- they do not yield to just any treatment --- yet the hymn has attracted tune-makers from every generation.

The nameless English tune of uncertain authorship (harmonized by Hubert P. Main) that accompanied the words in smaller old manuals remains the favorite in the memory of thousands who know it from Sunday school. Sir William Davenant's air for Moore's love-song "Believe Me, If All Those Endearing Young Charms" (the tune of "Fair Harvard") has also been used --- startling at first, but in keeping with the policy of Luther and Wesley of using beloved secular melodies for sacred words. John Henry Cornell wrote "St. Kevin" for it forty years before the book was published (1905), and the newest church collections have set it to "Athens," a Greek melody. The hymn has been sung to more different tunes than almost any other Sunday-school piece.

Mrs. Luke died in 1906 at the age of ninety-two, having outlived the century her hymn helped to define.
