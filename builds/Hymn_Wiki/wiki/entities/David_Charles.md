# David Charles of Carmarthen

David Charles was known in his generation not primarily as a hymnist but as a preacher --- a man whose mind was so persistently fixed on heaven that his contemporaries remembered him as heavenly-minded in an unusual and palpable sense. His hymns carry that same quality.

## Life

Charles was born in 1762 and died in 1834. He was a Calvinistic Methodist minister at Carmarthen, a town in southwest Wales with a long nonconformist history. He was the brother of Thomas Charles of Bala, whose work in establishing circulating schools and supporting the British and Foreign Bible Society made him one of the most significant figures in Welsh religious education. David's work was more local, more pastoral, and more purely devotional.

## "The Heights of Fair Salem Ascended"

This hymn envisions the ascent to the heavenly Jerusalem --- Salem, the city of peace --- with the confidence of a soul already oriented upward. The Heights of fair Salem are the heights of Zion in Revelation, and the hymn's movement is toward them: not as a hope in doubt but as a direction already established. It belongs to the tradition of Welsh heavenly-mindedness that runs from Williams of Pantycelyn through Ann Griffiths and into the nineteenth century.

## Character

Butterworth-Brown describe Charles as a beloved preacher whose heavenly-minded character was his defining quality. In Welsh Calvinistic Methodism, this was a recognized spiritual type --- the saint who had so thoroughly made peace with mortality and so thoroughly oriented his affections toward the eternal that his very presence was calming to those around him.
