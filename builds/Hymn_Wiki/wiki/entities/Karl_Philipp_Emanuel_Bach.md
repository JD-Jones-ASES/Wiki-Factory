# Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach

Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach — called "the Berlin Bach" to distinguish him from his father, the great Sebastian Bach of Saxe Weimar — was born in Weimar on March 14, 1714. He early devoted himself to music, and coming to Berlin when twenty-four years old was appointed Chamber Musician (Kammer Musicus) in the Royal Chapel, where he often accompanied Frederick the Great (who was an accomplished flutist) on the harpsichord.

His most numerous compositions were piano music, but he wrote a celebrated "Sanctus," two oratorios, and a number of chorals. He died in Hamburg on December 14, 1788.

## The Choral "Weimar"

Of his contributions to Christian hymnody, the most notable is his choral "Weimar," composed for the ancient Christmas hymn "All Praise to Thee, Eternal Lord" (*Gelobet Seist du Jesu Christ*). Butterworth-Brown note that the 18th-century tune "Weimar" from the *Evangelical Hymnal* "suits the spiritual tone of the hymn, and suggests the Gregorian dignity of its origin." The ancient hymn itself is variously ascribed to a 9th-century author or to Gregory the Great in the 6th century, though its German form is credited to Luther in most hymnals.

## Musical and Thematic Context

C.P.E. Bach stands in a lineage connecting the great classical Lutheran tradition to the emerging Enlightenment period. His father J.S. Bach gave the Protestant world its supreme statement of contrapuntal sacred music; his own work moved toward the lighter, more elegant galant style while retaining roots in the choral tradition. The tune "Weimar" is one of those pieces where this bridge is felt — it carries the austere weight of the ancient Christmas text while breathing the refinement of 18th-century harmonics.
