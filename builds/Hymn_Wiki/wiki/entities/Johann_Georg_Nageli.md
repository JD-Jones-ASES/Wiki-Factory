# Johann Georg Nägeli

Johann Georg Nägeli was born in Zurich, Switzerland, in 1768. He became a significant figure in European music publishing and composition, and was known also as a poet and educator. He died in 1836.

## His Hymn Tunes

Nägeli's compositions were mostly vocal, for school and church use. His melodies came into wide congregational use through the work of [[Lowell_Mason]], who adapted several of them for American hymnals. The most beloved of these adaptations is "Dennis," a "soft and smoothly modulated harmony" that Butterworth-Brown describe as having "no note out of sympathy" with the deep feeling of sacred brotherhood.

"Dennis" became the definitive setting for [[John_Fawcett]]'s "Blest Be the Tie That Binds" and was also used for [[Benjamin_Beddome]]'s "Did Christ O'er Sinners Weep?" Both hymns — one about fellowship, one about penitence — carry the same quality of quiet emotional depth that Nägeli's melody provides.

## The Beethoven Interpolation

Butterworth-Brown record a characteristic anecdote about Nägeli's irrepressible creative confidence. While publishing Beethoven's three great solo sonatas (Opus 31), he interpolated two bars of his own composition — an act much commented upon in musical circles. The authors note that this does not seem to have cost him Beethoven's friendship, suggesting, "like Murillo to the servant who meddled with his paintings, the great master forgave the liberty, because the work was so good."

It is a telling story: a man of such musical self-assurance that he would improve Beethoven, yet whose best-remembered contribution to posterity is a gentle choral that carries the simplest and most human of devotional sentiments.
