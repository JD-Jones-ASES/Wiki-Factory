# Ann Griffiths

Few figures in the entire history of Christian hymnody are as poignant as Ann Griffiths of Dolwar Fechan. She died at twenty-nine, and she had been a Christian for only eight years and a wife for only ten months. Yet the hymns she left behind --- transcribed mostly from memory by those who had heard her sing them --- have moved Welsh hearts for two centuries.

## A Romantic Figure

"She remains," said Dr. Parry, her fellow-countryman, "a romantic figure in the religious history of Wales. Her hymns leave upon the reader an undefinable impression both of sublimity and mysticism. Her brief life-history is most worthy of study both from a literary and a religious point of view."

Ann Griffiths was born in 1776 at Dolwar Fechan, Montgomeryshire. Her hymns grew from an experience of faith so vivid and so all-consuming that she could not always find words for it in ordinary speech. She turned instead to song.

## Her Earliest Hymn

Her earliest hymn is called her sweetest. More poetically translated than her later, more intense pieces, it came to her as she returned from an exciting service, filled with thoughts of her unworthiness and of the beauty of her Saviour. She turned down a sheltered lane to pray alone:

> My soul, behold the fitness
>   Of this great Son of God,
> Trust Him for life eternal
>   And cast on Him thy load,
> A man --- touched with the pity
>   Of every human woe,
> A God --- to claim the kingdom
>   And vanquish every foe.

By the time she reached home she had formed it into words. The later stanzas were written after the inspiration had been received and digested:

> Great Author of salvation
>   And providence for man,
> Thou rulest earth and heaven
>   With Thy far-reaching plan.
> Today or on the morrow,
>   Whatever woe betide,
> Grant us Thy strong assistance,
>   Within Thy hand to hide.

## "Love Eternal" and "Mysteries of Grace"

Among her other surviving hymns are two that show the depth of her theological imagination. "Here Behold the Tent of Meeting" (*Dyma Babell y cyfarfod*) is entitled "Love Eternal" and praises the divine plan to satisfy the Law and at the same time save the sinner:

> Here behold the tent of meeting,
>   In the blood a peace with heaven,
> Refuge from the blood-avengers,
>   For the sick a Healer given.
> Here the sinner nestles safely
>   At the very Throne divine,
> And Heaven's righteous law, all holy,
>   Still on him shall smile and shine.

"How Sweet the Covenant to Remember" (*Bydd melus gofio y cyfammod*), entitled "Mysteries of Grace," is a hymn of unusual literalness --- the kind that the Welsh call *hwyl*, that untranslatable word for holy fervour:

> He is the great Propitiation
>   Who with the thieves that anguish bare;
> He nerved the arms of His tormentors
>   To drive the nails that fixed Him there.
> While He discharged the sinner's ransom,
>   And made the Law in honor be,
> Righteousness shone undimmed, resplendent,
>   And me the Covenant set free.

## Death and Memorial

Ann Griffiths died in 1805 at the age of twenty-nine. In 1904, near the centennial of her death, amid the echoes of her own hymns, and the rising waves of the great revival sweeping over Wales, the people of Dolwar Fechan dedicated a new chapel to her name and to the glory of God. In the 1904--5 revival, which gathered eighty thousand souls in Wales, her hymns were sung again as if they had just been born.

The author of *Sweet Singers of Wales* compressed her whole story in a single line: "She had a Christian life of eight years and a married life of ten months."
