# Henry Hart Milman

Henry Hart Milman was born in 1791 and educated at Oxford, where in 1821 he was installed as Professor of Poetry. He was later appointed Dean of St. Paul's Cathedral in London, a position he held for many years. He was also a distinguished historian, author of *The History of the Jews* and *The History of Christianity*. He died on September 24, 1868, having lived a "laborious and useful life of seventy-seven years," as Butterworth-Brown put it.

## The Chariot! The Chariot!

Written while Milman was Oxford Professor of Poetry, under the title "The Last Day," this hymn is described by Butterworth-Brown as "not only a hymn, but a poem — a sublime ode that recalls, in a different movement, the tones of the *Dies Irae*." It was used at old revival meetings as a counterpoint to the severe Calvinism of earlier pieces — a vision of divine glory to move "sinners unmoved by the invisible God of Horeb":

> The Chariot! the Chariot! its wheels roll in fire
> When the Lord cometh down in the pomp of His ire,
> Lo, self-moving, it drives on its pathway of cloud,
> And the heavens with the burden of Godhead are bowed.

## Ride On, Ride On in Majesty

Milman's Palm Sunday processional — one of the finest in the English tradition — has been continuously sung since its publication and remains in most major hymnals.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Butterworth-Brown note that his hymn "When Our Heads Are Bowed with Woe" "may have been written soon after he laid three of his children in one grave, in the north aisle of Westminster Abbey" — one of the most painful domestic facts in the biography of any Victorian hymn-writer. The hymn is remarkable for its restraint, which makes its grief the more audible.

Milman was unusual among hymn-writers of his rank in combining literary eminence, scholarly distinction, and the highest ecclesiastical office. He represents the learned Anglican tradition at its most confident.
