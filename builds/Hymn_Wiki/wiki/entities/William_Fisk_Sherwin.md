# William Fisk Sherwin

William Fisk Sherwin (1826–1887) was an American composer of hymn tunes notable for their popular appeal and usefulness in Sunday school and church services.

## Life and Career

According to Nutter and Tillett, Sherwin was an American composer of hymn tunes "that are notably popular and useful in Sunday school and church services." He served as music director at the Chautauqua Institution in western New York, where he worked alongside hymn writer [[Mary_Artemisia_Lathbury]].

## Tunes in the Methodist Hymnal

Sherwin contributed two tunes to the Methodist Hymnal:

- **Evening Praise** — a tune associated with Lathbury's "Day is dying in the west"
- **Bread of Life** — composed for Lathbury's "Break thou the bread of life"

Nutter and Tillett note that "few tunes in this Hymnal are more admired than these two tunes by Mr. Sherwin."

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

The Chautauqua partnership between Sherwin and [[Mary_Artemisia_Lathbury]] produced two of the most beloved hymn-and-tune pairings in the Methodist Hymnal. Sherwin wrote both tunes specifically for Lathbury's texts, and the closeness of fit between text and tune is part of what made them endure. Nutter and Tillett's high praise — among the most admired tunes in the Hymnal — reflects the consensus of the committee that compiled it.
