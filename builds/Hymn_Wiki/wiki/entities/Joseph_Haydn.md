# Joseph Haydn

Joseph Haydn, son of a poor wheelwright, was born in 1732 in Rohron, a village on the borders of Hungary and Austria. His precocity of musical talent was such that he began composing at the age of ten. Prince Esterhazy discovered his genius when he was poor and friendless, and his fortune was made. As Music Master for the Prince's Private Chapel — a position he held for twenty years — he wrote many of the beautiful symphonies that placed him among the foremost composers in that form.

Invited to England, he received the Doctor's degree at Oxford, and there composed his great oratorio of "The Creation," besides his "Twelve Grand Symphonies" and a long list of minor works, secular and sacred. His invention was inexhaustible.

## A Pious Composer

Haydn was a sincerely pious man. When writing his great oratorio of "The Creation" at sixty-seven years of age, he said: "I knelt down every day and prayed God to strengthen me for my work." This daily spiritual preparation was similar to Handel's when creating his "Messiah." Butterworth-Brown remark that it may be said of sacred music as truly as of astronomy: "The undevout composer is mad."

Near Haydn's death in Vienna in 1809, when he heard for the last time his magnificent chorus "Let there be Light!" he exclaimed: "Not mine, not mine. It all came to me from above."

## Musical and Thematic Context

Haydn's contributions to hymnody include the tune "Haydn" — which appears in several important hymnals as the chosen music for William Goode's devout praise hymn "Crown His Head with Endless Blessing" — and the tune "Creation," used for Isaac Watts's "Be Thou Exalted, O My God." Of the latter Butterworth-Brown write that "Haydn furnished it out of his chorus of morning stars" and call it "a march of trumpets."

The tune "Lyons," most often sung to [[Robert_Grant]]'s "O Worship the King," is also associated with Haydn's name. Haydn's oratorio music also supplied the adaptation "Manoah" (from "The Creation"), used with Addison's great hymn "When All Thy Mercies, O My God."
