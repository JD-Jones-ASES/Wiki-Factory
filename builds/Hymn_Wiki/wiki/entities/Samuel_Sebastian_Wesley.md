# Samuel Sebastian Wesley

Samuel Sebastian Wesley was born in London in 1810, the grandson of [[Charles_Wesley]], the great hymn-writer of the Methodist revival. Like his father Samuel Wesley, he became a distinguished musician, serving as organist at Exeter, Winchester, and Gloucester Cathedrals. Oxford gave him the degree of Doctor of Music. He died in Gloucester on April 19, 1876.

## The Tune "Aurelia"

Butterworth-Brown note that Wesley's tune "Aurelia" was chosen by later hymnals for [[James_Montgomery]]'s "Hail to the Lord's Anointed," with a movement similar to that of the tune "Webb." "Aurelia" has become most universally known, however, as the setting for "The Church's One Foundation" — one of the great Victorian hymns of ecclesiology. The tune's broad, flowing character suits both the missionary exuberance of Montgomery and the doctrinal solidity of the later hymn.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Samuel Sebastian Wesley occupies an interesting position in hymn history: the grandson of the man who wrote more hymns than almost any other figure in Christian history, he himself made his mark not as a hymn-writer but as a composer of tunes. Where Charles Wesley gave the church words, Samuel Sebastian gave it music. The family gift ran through both streams of the Anglican-Methodist tradition, and "Aurelia" is its lasting cathedral-organ echo.

His compositions include numerous anthems, services, and other sacred pieces for choir and congregational singing, as well as instrumental melodies. He also composed "Harewood" and other tunes still found in Anglican hymnals.
