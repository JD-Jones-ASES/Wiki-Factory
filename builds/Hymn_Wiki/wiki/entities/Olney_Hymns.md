# Olney Hymns

The *Olney Hymns* of 1779 is one of the great hymn collections in English Christianity. Its 349 hymns were written by two very different men in the same small English village: [[John_Newton]], the former slave-ship captain turned curate, and [[William_Cowper]], the afflicted poet who lived on the edge of mental collapse. The contrast between them is part of what makes the collection extraordinary.

## The Partnership

Newton came to Olney as curate in 1764. He was a man of robust faith, his confidence in grace grounded in a dramatic personal history of sin and rescue. Cowper, invited to Olney in 1767, was the opposite: brilliant, sensitive, tormented by the fear that God had somehow passed him by.

Newton proposed the hymn-writing project partly as a way to occupy Cowper's increasingly troubled mind. The two men worked together for several years, Newton contributing the bulk of the hymns (283) and Cowper contributing 66. The collection was published in 1779 under the title *Olney Hymns, in Three Books.*

## The Hymns

The collection contains some of the most beloved hymns in the English language:

**From Newton:**
- **"Amazing grace! how sweet the sound"** --- the autobiography of a man who was once lost
- **"How sweet the Name of Jesus sounds"** --- ranked among the finest hymns of tenderness in English
- **"Glorious things of thee are spoken"** --- a hymn of the Church
- **"Come, my soul, thy suit prepare"**
- **"May the grace of Christ our Saviour"** --- beloved as a benediction

**From Cowper:**
- **"God moves in a mysterious way"** --- written after his near-suicide in 1773
- **"O for a closer walk with God"**
- **"Sometimes a light surprises"**
- **"There is a fountain filled with blood"** --- vivid Passion imagery that sparked theological controversy
- **"Jesus, where'er Thy people meet"**
- **"In holy contemplation"**

## The Human Stories Behind the Hymns

"Amazing Grace" is inseparable from Newton's life --- the infidel, the libertine, the servant of slavers, pardoned and restored. He wrote it out of the depths of his own experience.

"God moves in a mysterious way" is inseparable from Cowper's 1773 crisis, when he ordered a post-chaise to drive him to the River Ouse to drown himself, and found a stranger sitting at the exact place where he had planned to leap. The hymn about divine providence was written by a man who had just been saved from himself by an act of providence he did not yet understand.

## Legacy

The *Olney Hymns* represent Anglican Evangelical hymnody at its richest. The collection reflects the full spectrum of genuine Christian experience: Newton's confident praise, Cowper's trembling faith, the struggle and the triumph, the darkness and the light. Together they demonstrate that God can work through very different instruments, and that the broken vessel and the bold vessel may both be needed.
