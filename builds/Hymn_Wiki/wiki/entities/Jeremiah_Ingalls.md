# Jeremiah Ingalls

Jeremiah Ingalls was born March 1, 1764, at Andover, Massachusetts, the eldest child of Abijah Ingalls. His father died when Jeremiah was thirteen, a casualty of the privations of the Revolutionary War. Soon after reaching his majority, Ingalls settled in Newbury, Vermont; married Mary Bigelow (also from Massachusetts) on April 28, 1791; and in 1800 built a two-story house in which he kept a tavern for ten or more years. At various times he worked as farmer, cooper, or singing master. He moved to Rochester, Vermont in 1819, then to Hancock, Vermont, where he died April 6, 1828, at sixty-four.

## The Composer

Ingalls had a high voice, was expert on the bass viol, and a ready reader of music. He was a deacon of the Congregational Church and leader of his choir. His best tunes — "New Jerusalem" and "Northfield" — were introduced in the Newbury sanctuary from manuscript copies. "Northfield" is said to have been composed at an inn in Northfield, New Hampshire, while the author was waiting an unusually long time for dinner.

He also composed words and music for special occasions, including an "Election Hymn," an "Election Ode," and a funeral piece titled "An Acrostic on Judith Brock."

## Christian Harmony

His collection *The Christian Harmony* (144 pages, printed by Henry Ranlet at Exeter, New Hampshire) preserved much of the "old-fashioned conference meeting music" of his era. Later compilers attributed many tunes to his authorship; his "Lavonia" and "Pennsylvania" were popular for years. The "Pennyroyal Hymns" — as Vermont folk remembered them — bore his stamp.

"Northfield" survived into modern hymnals as his most popular tune. "Come, Ye Sinners" (sometimes called "Invitation"), "Filmore," and "Kentucky" appeared in *The Methodist Hymnal* of 1878. The words set to his tunes varied widely across different books, as Ingalls had not written his music for any definite text. (Metcalf, p. 122-124)

His widow returned to Rochester, Vermont, after his death and died there April 18, 1848.
