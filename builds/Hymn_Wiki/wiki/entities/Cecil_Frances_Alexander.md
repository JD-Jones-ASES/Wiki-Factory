# Cecil Frances Alexander

Cecil Frances Alexander wrote for children, and in doing so wrote for everyone. Her hymns for little children turned out to be hymns for the entire church --- the kind of verse that starts in the nursery and stays in the memory until death.

## Life

She was born in Dublin in 1823 and grew up in Ireland. She married William Alexander, who became Bishop of Derry (and later Archbishop of Armagh), and she spent her life in the Anglo-Irish church world, surrounded by the pastoral realities of parish ministry. Her husband's prominence in the church gave her work an immediate audience, but her hymns outlasted every institutional advantage.

She died in 1895.

## Hymns for Little Children

Her collection *Hymns for Little Children*, published in 1848, was one of the most successful hymnbooks of the nineteenth century. Its declared purpose was to make the articles of the Apostles' Creed accessible to young minds through poetry --- a catechetical ambition that produced art of lasting power.

## "There Is a Green Hill Far Away"

This hymn, written for *Hymns for Little Children*, meditates on the crucifixion in language a child can understand without simplifying what it says. It does not explain the atonement theoretically but presents it dramatically: a green hill outside a city wall, a death that we could not have paid ourselves, a faith that we must believe to be saved. George Coles Stebbins composed a tune for it that suited the hymn's quiet gravity.

Butterworth-Brown note that the "green hill" has no wall --- the grammar means "a city wall" outside which the hill stands, not a wall on the hill --- a grammatical point that charmed early readers who took it as evidence of the hymn's natural voice.

## The Other Hymns

"Once in Royal David's City" and "All Things Bright and Beautiful" came from the same collection and the same instinct: to make the great truths of Christian faith sing in a child's register without condescension or false simplification. "All Things Bright and Beautiful," with its inventory of creation's beauties, became one of the most-translated hymns in English.

## Legacy

Cecil Frances Alexander represents the finest achievement of Victorian children's hymnody. Where many writers in that tradition were merely pretty, she was theologically serious. Where others were serious, they were often grim. She managed to be both beautiful and true, and that is the rarest combination in the hymnbook.
