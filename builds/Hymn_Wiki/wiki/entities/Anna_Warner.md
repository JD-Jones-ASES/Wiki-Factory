# Anna Bartlett Warner

Anna Warner and her sister Susan were known in literary circles under the pen names Amy Lothrop and Elizabeth Wetherell, but to the hymnbook tradition they are known simply as the women who lived at Martlaer's Rock, West Point, and gave their lives to the cadets across the river.

## Life at West Point

Anna was born in 1820 in what Butterworth-Brown identify as Martlaer, near West Point, New York. She and her sister Susan lived together through much of their adult lives on an island property just across from the Military Academy at West Point. For decades they conducted a Sunday school for the cadets --- an act of faithful teaching that continued season after season, year after year.

She lived to ninety-five and was buried with military honors at West Point, a tribute from the institution she had served without appointment or salary for so long.

## "One More Day's Work for Jesus"

This hymn of consecration was among Anna's contributions to the gospel song tradition. It takes the posture of a soul reviewing each day's service to Christ and finding it a gift rather than a burden --- each day's work another opportunity, not another obligation. It belongs to the same tradition of active-devotion hymns that characterized the evangelical movement of the later nineteenth century.

## "Jesus Loves Me"

The words "Jesus loves me, this I know" appeared first not in a hymn but in a novel --- *Say and Seal* (1860), co-written by Susan Warner, in which a dying child is comforted with the words. Anna wrote them for the novel, but William Bradbury set them to the tune that carried them to every continent. The hymn became the world's most recognized children's song in Christianity.
