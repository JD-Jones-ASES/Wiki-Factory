# Francis Scott Key

Francis Scott Key, lawyer, was born on the estate of his father, John Ross Key, in Frederick, Maryland, on August 1, 1779. He died in Baltimore on January 11, 1843. A bronze statue over his grave and another in Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, represent the nationality of his fame and the gratitude of a whole land.

Though a slaveholder by inheritance, Key deplored the existence of human slavery and not only originated a scheme of African colonization but did all that a model master could do for the enslaved people on his plantation, in compliance with the Scripture command. He helped them in their family troubles, defended them gratuitously in the courts, and held regular Sunday-school services for them.

Educated at St. John's College and an active member of the Episcopal Church, he was not only a scholar but a devout and exemplary man.

## Lord, with Glowing Heart I'd Praise Thee

Key wrote this noble hymn in 1817 — the work of the author of "The Star-Spangled Banner," and proof that the national poet was no less a Christian than a patriot. Butterworth-Brown call it "a noble American hymn of which the country may well be proud":

> Lord, with glowing heart I'd praise Thee
>   For the bliss Thy love bestows,
> For the pardoning grace that saves me,
>   And the peace that from it flows.

> Let Thy grace, my soul's chief treasure,
>   Love's pure flame within me raise,
> And, since words can never measure,
>   Let my life show forth Thy praise.

## Musical and Thematic Context

The tune "St. Chad" — a choral in D with a four-bar unison, from the *Evangelical Hymnal* — is assigned to this hymn by Butterworth-Brown and described as "worthy of the hymn." The composer, Richard Redhead, was organist of the Church of St. Mary Magdalene, Paddington, born at Harrow, Middlesex on March 1, 1820, and educated at Magdalene College, Oxford. He published the *Laudes Dominæ* Gregorian Psalter in 1843 and a Book of Tunes for the *Christian Year*.
