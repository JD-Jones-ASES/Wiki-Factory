# William Chatterton Dix

William Chatterton Dix (1837–1898) was an eminent English author and hymn writer whose contributions to hymnody were valued for their warmth and theological substance.

## Life and Career

Dix was born at Bristol on June 14, 1837. He was manager of a marine insurance company in Glasgow. His contributions to hymnody were valuable; some twenty or thirty of them were in common use in English and American hymnals.

Among his best-known volumes were:
- Hymns of Love and Joy (1861)
- A Sailor's Songs (1867)
- Vision of All Saints (1871)
- Seekers of a City (1878)

Many of his hymns were contributed to Hymns Ancient and Modern and other English hymnals. He also published The Risen Christ (1863) and The Patient Herd. He died in 1898 (OCR uncertain on exact year).

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

Three of Dix's hymns appear in the Methodist Hymnal:

- "As with gladness men of old" (Epiphany hymn)
- "Come unto me, ye weary"
- "Alleluia! sing to Jesus"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett note that Dix was unusual among Victorian hymn writers in combining an active commercial career (marine insurance) with serious hymnic writing. His Epiphany hymn "As with gladness men of old," composed reportedly during an illness, became one of the standard English Epiphany hymns used across all traditions. The range of his three hymns in the Methodist Hymnal — from Epiphany narrative to invitation to eucharistic praise — shows the breadth of his hymnic output.
