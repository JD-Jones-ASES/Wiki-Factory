# Henry Kirke White

Henry Kirke White burned briefly and brightly. He died at barely twenty years old --- a young man of exceptional gifts who had just enough time to find faith and write a handful of hymns before his short life ended. The story of how he found that faith became as celebrated as anything he wrote.

## Life and Conversion

White was born in Nottingham in 1795, the son of a butcher, and showed early gifts as a writer and scholar. The story of his conversion involves a friend named Almond who had turned to atheism and was working to draw White with him. Almond came to White late one night, having found nothing in unbelief to sustain him, and the two young men talked until dawn. By morning both had found their way to Christ. White's faith, once established, burned with the intensity characteristic of gifted converts.

He went up to Cambridge with great ambitions and the financial support of patrons, but the combination of poverty, overwork, and constitutional weakness proved fatal. He died in the autumn of 1806, barely past his twenty-first year.

## "When Marshalled on the Nightly Plain"

This hymn meditates on the star that led the Magi to Bethlehem, transforming that astronomical image into a meditation on the light that guides souls to Christ. It has a quality of elevated wondering that marks the best of the early-Romantic evangelical hymns --- the sense of a mind and a faith working at full stretch.

Robert Southey, the Poet Laureate, edited and published White's remains after his death, and the preface Southey wrote for that volume ensured that White's story reached a wide audience. His brief life became a kind of martyrdom narrative for the evangelical movement --- evidence that faith could be real and consuming even in the most gifted minds.
