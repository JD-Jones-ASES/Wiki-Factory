# Nicolaus Selnecker

## Champion of Lutheran Doctrine

Nicolaus Selnecker lived in the tumultuous generation after the Reformation --- a time when Lutheran doctrine was being attacked from without (by Rome and by the Calvinists) and disputed from within. He became one of the great champions of pure Lutheran teaching and left his mark both as a theologian and as a hymn-writer.

## Biographical Sketch

Born in 1532 at Hersbruck, Germany, Selnecker showed early artistic gifts. By age twelve he was organist at the chapel in the Kaiserburg at Nuremberg. He later studied law at Wittenberg, where he came under the influence of Philip Melanchthon and redirected himself toward ministry. He was said to be Melanchthon's favorite student.

His career was a series of displacements, each resulting from his refusal to compromise on Lutheran doctrine. He served as second court preacher at Dresden, was removed for defending Lutheran teaching against the court's Calvinist tendencies, spent three years at Jena, returned to favor and was appointed to the theology chair at Leipzig in 1568, was again embroiled in doctrinal disputes, and eventually returned to Leipzig only to die there five days after his return in 1592. He was worn out by a lifetime of controversy.

## The Formula of Concord

His most lasting theological contribution was co-authoring the Formula of Concord (1577), the final and definitive statement of Lutheran doctrine, which clarified the Lutheran position on disputed questions and brought much of the post-Reformation theological controversy to a close.

## Hymns

Selnecker wrote about 150 hymns, many of which reflect his personal storms. His most beloved is **"Let Me Be Thine Forever"**, which began as a single stanza prayer of trust, probably written during one of his most grievous experiences. Two additional stanzas by an unknown author were added nearly a century later, and in its full three-stanza form the hymn became a beloved Lutheran confirmation hymn:

> *Let me be Thine forever,*
> *My gracious God and Lord,*
> *May I forsake Thee never,*
> *Nor wander from Thy Word.*

His other well-known hymn, **"Abide with Us, O Saviour Dear"**, directly reflects the doctrinal distress of his times. One stanza reads:

> *This is a dark and evil day,*
> *Forsake us not, O Lord, we pray;*
> *And let us in our grief and pain*
> *Thy Word and sacraments retain.*

Selnecker also built up the famous Motet Choir of St. Thomas Church in Leipzig, the choir that was later conducted by Johann Sebastian Bach.
