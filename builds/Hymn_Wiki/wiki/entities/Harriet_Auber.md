# Harriet Auber

Miss Harriet Auber was a daughter of James Auber of London and was born in that city on October 4, 1773. After leaving London she led a secluded life at Broxbourne and Hoddesdon, in Hertfordshire, writing devotional poetry, sacred songs, and psalm paraphrases. She died in Hoddesdon on January 20, 1862.

Her *Spirit of the Psalms*, published in 1829, was a collection of lyrics founded on the biblical Psalms. She lived to witness and sympathize with the pioneer missionary enterprise of the nineteenth century and — although she could not stand in the battle-line — was happy to have written hymns that those in the front lines loved to sing.

## Hasten, Lord, the Glorious Time

This melodious missionary hymn draws from Psalm 72, one of the great Messianic psalms:

> Hasten, Lord, the glorious time
>   When beneath Messiah's sway
> Every nation, every clime
>   Shall the Gospel call obey.

The tune that best interprets it "in spirit and in living music," according to Butterworth-Brown, is [[Lowell_Mason]]'s "Eltham" — described as harmonious "like a chime of bells."

## Musical and Thematic Context

Butterworth-Brown note an interesting bibliographical puzzle: Julian's *Dictionary of Hymns and Hymn-writers* credits "With Joy We Hail the Sacred Day" to both Miss Auber and Henry Francis Lyte, though one writer was dead before the other's works were published. This may indicate coincident first lines — a known phenomenon in hymn history — or a collector who saw a forgotten hymn of Lyte's beginning with the same words.

Miss Auber's most enduring hymn may be the Pentecost hymn "Our Blest Redeemer, Ere He Breathed / His tender last farewell," which became a standard for Whitsuntide services in the Anglican tradition.
