# James Lyon

James Lyon holds a distinguished place at the beginning of American sacred music. When he published *Urania* in Philadelphia in 1761, he produced the first substantial collection of psalm tunes compiled by a native American musician --- and one that contained original compositions, not merely English reprints.

## The Making of Urania

Lyon was born in Newark, New Jersey, on July 1, 1735. He graduated from the College of New Jersey (now Princeton University) in 1759, and the following year was working in Philadelphia while continuing his studies. In 1761 his collection appeared: *Urania, or a Choice Collection of Psalm-tunes, Anthems, and Hymns, from the Most Approved Authors, with Some Entirely New*, printed by William Bradford of Philadelphia.

The book was offered by subscription and contained 141 subscribers, drawn largely from Lyon's college connections, who spread copies throughout the colonies from Georgia to New England. Three or four editions were eventually published. The tune known as "Whitefield's," which Lyon set to the words "Come, Thou Almighty King," is what we now call "America" --- the tune of "God Save the King." Its appearance in *Urania* is one of its earliest printings in the colonies. (Metcalf, p. 36)

Lyon received his degree of A.M. from Princeton in 1762, was licensed by the Presbytery of New Brunswick in 1764, and ordained to preach. He served for a time in Nova Scotia, married Martha Holden in 1768, and then in 1771 accepted a call to the remote settlement of Machias, Maine.

## Minister, Patriot, and Farmer

Machias was frontier territory, and Lyon's life there was demanding. He and his congregation depended on the lumber trade for their food supply, and when the Revolutionary War interrupted commerce, Lyon dug clams and fished to feed his family. He was a patriot, "fighting as well as preaching" according to Metcalf. In 1782 a Congregational church was formally organized in Machias, and Lyon became its minister.

He remained in Machias until his death on December 25, 1794, at the age of fifty-nine. Seven children were born to him there, in addition to the two born in Nova Scotia.

## Legacy

*Urania* was a landmark because it included tunes marked as new and original --- the first such American compositions in print. Lyon was a musician of real ambition who gave America its first serious sacred music collection, even as he spent most of his life serving one of the most remote congregations in the new nation. His music did not outlast his generation, but the path he opened was traveled by every American church composer who came after him.
