# Edward John Hopkins

Edward John Hopkins was born at Westminster, London, on June 30, 1818. Beginning as a member of the Chapel Royal boy choir, he became organist of the Mitcham Church, Surrey, and afterwards of the Temple Church, London — one of the most distinguished positions in English church music. He received his Doctor of Music degree from the Archbishop of Canterbury in 1882.

## The Tune "St. Philip"

Hopkins's hymn tune "St. Philip" is the more modern piece assigned to [[William_Hammond]]'s revival hymn "Awake and Sing the Song of Moses and the Lamb." Butterworth-Brown describe it as the representative modern musical interpretation of that hymn.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Hopkins belongs to the generation of English church musicians — alongside [[Henry_Smart]], [[John_B_Dykes]], and William Henry Monk — that defined the Victorian-era sound of Anglican hymn-singing. His long tenure at the Temple Church gave him influence over one of the most musically distinguished congregations in England, and his work as a composer and teacher contributed to the refinement of congregational psalmody that characterized the best English hymnals of the second half of the 19th century.
