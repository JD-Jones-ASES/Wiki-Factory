# Peter Cartwright

Peter Cartwright was born in Amherst County, Virginia, on September 1, 1785, and died near Pleasant Plains, Sangamon County, Illinois, in September 1872. He was one of the most legendary circuit-preachers of early American Methodism: physically formidable, spiritually indomitable, and possessed of a gift for exuberant outdoor preaching that made him a figure of enduring myth in the frontier church.

## Singing Songs of Revival

Butterworth-Brown record Cartwright's most memorable musical moment with characteristic directness. A "strange, wild paean of exultant song" was often heard from him — "a remembered fragment shows its quality":

> Then my soul mounted higher
>   In a chariot of fire,
> And the moon it was under my feet.

There is a tradition that he sang this over a "stalwart blacksmith while chastising him for an ungodly defiance and assault in the course of one of his gospel journeys — and that the defeated blacksmith became his friend and follower."

## Musical and Thematic Context

Cartwright belongs to the tradition of the frontier evangelist who brought the church to people who had no church — not in buildings but in fields, forests, and camp-meetings. His hymnody was not composed but improvised and remembered: the product of a living oral tradition that fed the revivals of the early nineteenth century.

His career overlapped with the great era of American camp-meeting religion, and he lived to see the frontier religion he had helped establish become the established religion of the American heartland. His memoirs — *Autobiography of Peter Cartwright* — remain a primary source for the religious history of the early American republic.
