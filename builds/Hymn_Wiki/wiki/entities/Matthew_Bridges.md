# Matthew Bridges

Matthew Bridges (1800–1894) was an English hymn writer best known for "Crown him with many crowns," one of the most widely sung coronation hymns in the Christian tradition.

## Life and Career

Bridges was born at Maldon, Essex, England, on July 14, 1800. He was educated in the Church of England but became a convert to the Church of Rome in connection with the famous Tractarian movement led by Cardinal Newman and others. For several years before his death he resided in the province of Quebec, Canada, where he died on October 6, 1894.

He was the author of several books, the most valuable of which is Hymns of the Heart (1848).

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

Three of Bridges's hymns appear in the Methodist Hymnal:

- "Crown him with many crowns"
- "My God, accept my heart this day"
- "Rise, glorious Conqueror, rise"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett note that Bridges's journey from the Church of England to Rome — part of the Tractarian wave that also brought [[John_Henry_Newman]] into Catholicism — did not prevent his hymns from finding wide Protestant use. "Crown him with many crowns" has proven one of the most enduring coronation texts in English hymnody, sung across all denominations. His Hymns of the Heart (1848) was the primary source for his hymnary.
