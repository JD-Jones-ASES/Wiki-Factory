# Samuel Pearce

Samuel Pearce lived only thirty-three years, but in those years he helped light the fire of modern Baptist foreign missions — and left behind two hymns that testify to the depth of devotion that animated everything he did.

## Life

Pearce was born on the coast of England at Plymouth, Devonshire, July 20, 1766. At fifteen he was deeply impressed with his need of a Savior; a year later, through a sermon, he was led to Christ. There was a period of wandering, then a permanent return to faith — he gave himself to his Master's service "reverently, in an everlasting covenant."

He went to the Baptist College at Bristol to study for the ministry, devoting himself meanwhile to evangelistic work in and around Bristol. In 1790, he became pastor of the Cannon Street Baptist church in Birmingham, ordained August 18 — the ordaining prayer offered by Andrew Fuller, the charge by Dr. Caleb Evans, the sermon preached by Robert Hall senior from Deuteronomy 1:38: "Encourage him."

He labored in Birmingham with great zeal and success until his death, October 10, 1799.

## The Baptist Missionary Society

Pearce was one of the co-founders of the organization that launched modern Protestant missions. His name is affixed to the resolutions adopted at the meeting of ministers at Kettering, October 2, 1792, at which "The Baptist Society for Propagating the Gospel among the Heathen" was organized. He was also one of the contributors that day to the new Society's funds.

William Carey sailed for India in 1793. Pearce's desire to go with him was so powerful — and his convictions so strong — that he placed the question before the Baptist Missionary Society's Board: should he go to India, or remain in Birmingham? The Board advised him to stay; his congregation needed him, and he could do more for missions from England than from India. He yielded to their judgment, but never let go of the vision. In 1794, in a letter to Dr. Rogers of Philadelphia, he urged the formation of an American Baptist Foreign Missionary Society — a vision that would be realized, partly through the dramatic conversion of [[Adoniram_Judson]] to Baptist principles on a voyage to India in 1812.

## His Hymns

Andrew Fuller, who wrote a biography of Pearce, attributed to him the hymn

*In the floods of tribulation*

written in 1799. The hymn appeared in [[John_Rippon]]'s *Selection* (edition of 1800), and also in Bickersteth's *Christian Psalmody*, in Spurgeon's *Our Own Hymn Book*, and in an American supplement to Winchell's Watts. A second hymn, *The fabric of nature is fair* (fifteen verses), appears only in Rippon and was written in the author's sick chamber not long before he died.

Both hymns reflect the character of the man: steady confidence in God amid difficulty, a heart prepared for suffering, and joy that does not depend on comfortable circumstances.

## Baptist Tradition Context (Burrage)

Burrage notes that Dr. Belcher called these two hymns "sweet compositions" and described them as probably the last things retained from later editions of Rippon's *Selection* by a writer of Pearce's generation. The hymn *In the floods of tribulation* — with its refrain "Hallelujah! praise the Lord" — has the quality of a man writing from genuine experience of hardship and genuine experience of consolation.
