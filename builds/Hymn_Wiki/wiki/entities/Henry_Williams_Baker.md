# Henry Williams Baker

Sir Henry Williams Baker (1821–1877) was an eminent Church of England clergyman and hymn writer, best known as the editor-in-chief of Hymns Ancient and Modern and as the author of "The King of love my Shepherd is."

## Life and Career

Baker was the son of Sir Henry L. Baker and was born in London on May 27, 1821. He was educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, graduating B.A. in 1844. He took holy orders in 1844 and became vicar of Monkland, Herefordshire, in 1851, which benefice he held until his death. He succeeded to the baronetcy in 1851.

He is best known as editor-in-chief of Hymns Ancient and Modern, to which he contributed several of his own hymns.

## Character of His Hymns

According to Dr. Julian, quoted by Nutter and Tillett: "Of his hymns four only are in the highest strain of jubilation, another four are bright and cheerful, and the remainder are very tender but exceedingly plaintive, sometimes even to sadness." The language of his hymns is smooth and simple, the thought is correct and sometimes very beautifully expressed.

Baker died on February 12, 1877. His last audible words were a quotation of the third stanza of his own rendering of the twenty-third Psalm, "The King of love my Shepherd is."

## Hymns and Tunes in the Methodist Hymnal

Baker contributed both hymns and tunes:

- "O God of love, O King of Peace"
- "O perfect life of love"
- "The King of love my Shepherd is"
- Tunes: Stephanos, Hesperus

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett treat Baker as one of the central figures of Victorian Anglican hymnody, positioned at the intersection of the Tractarian movement (through Hymns Ancient and Modern) and a more evangelical warmth of feeling. The story of his death — quoting his own psalm paraphrase — gave that hymn a special posthumous resonance. His tunes Stephanos and Hesperus also appear in the Methodist Hymnal, making him one of the rare figures who contributed as both author and composer.
