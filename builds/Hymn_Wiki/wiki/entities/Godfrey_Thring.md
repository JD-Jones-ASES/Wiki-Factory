# Godfrey Thring

Godfrey Thring (1823–1903) was a Church of England clergyman and prolific hymn writer, one of the substantial figures in Victorian English hymnody.

## Life and Career

The son of J. G. D. Thring, Godfrey Thring was born at Alford on March 25, 1823. He graduated at Oxford in 1845, served different charges as curate and rector from 1846 to 1867, and was Rural Dean from 1867 to 1876. He served as Prebendary of Wells Cathedral from 1876 until his death on September 13, 1903.

He wrote many hymns, about twenty-five of which appear in different Church hymnals in England and America. He published:

- Hymns Congregational and Others (1866)
- Hymns and Verses (1866)
- Hymns and Sacred Lyrics (1874)
- Church of England Hymn Book (1880; second edition, 1882)

## Character of His Hymns

According to Dr. Julian (quoted by Nutter and Tillett), Thring's hymns "are mainly objective, and are all of them of a strong and decided character. In some of his finer hymns his tone is high and his structure massive; in several others his plaintiveness is very tender, whilst very varied, and his rhythm is almost always perfect. The prominent features throughout are a clear vision, a firm faith, a positive reality, and an exulting hopefulness."

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

Three of Thring's hymns appear in the Methodist Hymnal:

- "Fierce raged the tempest o'er the deep"
- "I saw the holy city"
- "Saviour, blessed Saviour"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

Nutter and Tillett treat Thring as one of the solid mid-rank Victorian hymn writers — not as celebrated as [[William_Walsham_How]] or [[John_Keble]], but possessing real craft and theological confidence. His three entries in the Methodist Hymnal represent the range from dramatic narrative ("Fierce raged the tempest") to eschatological vision to devotional supplication.
