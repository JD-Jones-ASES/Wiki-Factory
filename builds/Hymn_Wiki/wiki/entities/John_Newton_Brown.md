# John Newton Brown

John Newton Brown is now best known in connection with the New Hampshire Declaration of Faith, which has been very extensively adopted by Baptist churches across the country. As chairman of the committee that presented the Declaration, he performed valuable service in its preparation — but he was also a hymn writer and editor of wide influence.

## Life

Brown was born in New London, Connecticut, June 29, 1803. When fourteen years of age he was baptized at Hudson, New York. He entered Hamilton Theological Seminary and was graduated in 1823, ordained at Buffalo in 1824.

A series of pastorates followed: Providence, Rhode Island (1825, assisting Dr. Gano at the First Baptist church); Malden, Massachusetts (1827); Exeter, New Hampshire (1829). In 1838 he became associate professor of theology and pastoral relations in the New Hampton Literary and Theological Institution, remaining until 1845 when he became pastor of the Baptist church in Lexington, Virginia.

Ill health forced his withdrawal from the pastorate in 1849, and he became connected with the American Baptist Publication Society as editorial secretary, also editing *The Christian Chronicle* and *The National Baptist*. During his residence at Exeter he had edited his *Encyclopaedia of Religious Knowledge*, published at Brattleborough in 1835 and republished in England. He also published *Introduction to the History of Baptist Martyrs*, *Life and Times of Simon Menno* (1853), *History of the Baptist Publication Society* (1856), and *Descriptive Catalogue of the American Baptist Publication Society* (1861). At the time of his death he was preparing a history of the Baptists. He died at Germantown, Pennsylvania, May 14, 1868.

## His Hymns

Five of Brown's hymns appear in Freeman's *Selection of Hymns* (1829):

- "Unto him, unto him who is able to keep"
- "Welcome, welcome, dearest brothers"
- "Friends for whom a Savior died"
- "Have we all here met again?"
- "My friends, the parting hour is come"

Three more appear in Phineas Stowe's *Ocean Melodies*:

- "Come sinner! at our Lord's command"
- "And wilt thou stoop, Great God, so low"
- "O Thou! the high and lofty One"

The following hymn, found in *The Psalmist* and other collections, is characteristic of his style:

*Go, spirit of the sainted dead, / Go to thy longed-for, happy home; / The tears of man are o'er thee shed; / The voice of angels bids thee come.*

## Baptist Tradition Context (Burrage)

Burrage's note that Brown was "named after" the famous [[Adoniram_Judson]] (John Newton being Judson's middle name — he was christened Adoniram after a family member) underscores the degree to which Judson had become a symbolic figure for American Baptists by 1803. Brown's career as editor, theologian, and denominational servant represents the institutional maturation of American Baptist life in the middle nineteenth century.
