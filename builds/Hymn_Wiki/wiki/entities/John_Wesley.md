# John Wesley

**John Wesley** (1703-1791) --- English theologian, founder of Methodism; prolific hymn writer and translator.

Author of 1 hymn(s) in [[The_Christian_Hymn_Book|The Christian Hymn Book]] (1870). Further biographical information needed.

### From Lorenz (1938)

Lorenz devotes significant attention to John Wesley in Chapter XVI, portraying him as the organizer and editor of Methodist hymnody rather than its primary creative voice. Born at Epworth in 1703, John inherited his mother Susanna's organizing and administrative ability. Lorenz compares his role to what Calvin was for Reformed psalmody: "its initiator and director." He added critical power and a practical sense that his younger brother Charles lacked --- Charles wrote the hymns and John winnowed and edited them.

Lorenz traces three formative influences on John's hymnic vision: the family home at Epworth (which was "a nest of singing birds"), Isaac Watts's hymns (half of John's 1737 *Charleston Collection* consisted of Watts's hymns), and the Moravian brethren whose deep spirituality and genuine singing profoundly impressed him during the Atlantic voyage and at Herrnhut. As a hymn writer, John succeeded better as a translator than an original hymnist, producing important translations from the German of [[Gerhard_Tersteegen|Tersteegen]], [[Paul_Gerhardt|Gerhardt]], Scheffler, Spangenberg, and Zinzendorf. John's "keen and critical literary faculty" was brought to bear on both the cultural and spiritual aspects of hymnody, and his drastic corrections and revisions lifted the hymnody of his age to a higher literary plane. Lorenz notes that John did not encourage others to write hymns and even warned editors: "Hymn-cobblers should not try to mend them."
