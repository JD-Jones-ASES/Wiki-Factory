# Frank Mason North

Frank Mason North (1850–1935) was a minister of the Methodist Episcopal Church, a missions executive, and a hymn writer best known for the urban social gospel hymn "Where cross the crowded ways of life."

## Life and Career

North was born in New York on December 3, 1850. He graduated at Wesleyan University in 1872 and entered the ministry the same year. After filling important stations in the Methodist Episcopal Church for twenty years, he became in 1892 Corresponding Secretary of the New York City Church Extension and Missionary Society. Since that date he also served as editor of the Christian City, published in New York City.

## Hymns in the Methodist Hymnal

Two of North's hymns appear in the Methodist Hymnal:

- "Jesus, the calm that fills my breast"
- "Where cross the crowded ways of life"

## Methodist Hymnal Context (Nutter)

According to Nutter and Tillett, North's home mission hymn "Where cross the crowded ways of life" (No. 423) is "one of the best in the entire Hymnal." Written out of his direct experience of urban missionary work in New York City, it brings the social gospel impulse into hymnic form with unusual poetic force. The hymn stands alongside [[Washington_Gladden]]'s "O Master, let me walk with thee" as one of the great hymns of engaged Christian social conscience from the late nineteenth century.
