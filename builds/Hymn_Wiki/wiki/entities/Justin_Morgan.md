# Justin Morgan

Justin Morgan is remembered by two quite different communities: music historians know him as a singing master and tune composer, while horse enthusiasts know him as the breeder of the founding sire of the Morgan horse breed. He was genuinely both.

## Life and Character

Morgan was born in 1747 at West Springfield, Massachusetts. His health was delicate from early manhood, and he was unable to do sustained physical labor. Instead, he supported himself as a teacher --- writing schools, singing schools, and common district schools --- while also running a small tavern and dealing in horses. According to Metcalf, he was "greatly liked wherever he went on account of his urbane manners and upright character." (Metcalf, p. 67)

In 1788 he moved his family to Randolph, Vermont. His wife Martha died in March 1791, ten days after the birth of their fourth child, and the family was scattered among neighbors. Morgan never reassembled them. He served as town clerk of Randolph from 1790 to 1793, then fell into declining health. He died on March 2, 1798, at the age of fifty, leaving an estate valued at only $160.13.

Mahlon Cottrell, who drove the stage from Royalton to Montpelier, recalled often meeting Morgan "on the original Morgan horse going to his singing schools" --- a vivid image of the man in his element.

## Music

Morgan composed many tunes, including a "Judgement Anthem" that Metcalf describes as remarkable. His tune "Montgomery" appeared as late as 1849 in Leonard Marshall's *The Antiquarian*. He left a book of manuscript music. None of his compositions survived into modern use, but they were a real part of the sacred musical life of rural New England and Vermont in the Revolutionary era.
