# Hans Adolph Brorson

The hymn "Behold a host, arrayed in white" was first given to the world after its writer, Hans Adolph Brorson, had gone to join the host it describes. He wrote it in the last year of his life, part of a collection of seventy hymns published by his son in 1765 under the title *Hans Adolph Brorson's Swan-Song* --- a title perfectly chosen, for the hymn is exactly that.

## The Pietist Bishop

Brorson was born June 20, 1694, at Randrup, Denmark. He was a product of the great Pietistic movement emanating from Halle, Germany --- that awakening which swept through the Lutheran Church in the early eighteenth century and emphasized personal faith, prayer, and the new birth. His father was a Lutheran pastor, and all three of his sons entered the service of the Church.

Brorson's first pastorate was in his native city of Randrup, a place he dearly loved and often returned to in later life when oppressed by cares. He speaks of the eight years spent there as the happiest in his life.

## The Christmas Hymns of Tonder

In 1729 he was called as the Danish preacher at Tonder, a town with a mixed Danish and German population. A curious situation had developed in the church there: although Brorson preached in Danish, the congregation sang in German! To remedy this, Brorson in 1732 wrote a number of his famous Christmas hymns in Danish, among them *Den yndigste Rose er funden* ("The sweetest, the fairest of roses"), one of the most exquisite gems in sacred poetry.

From this time he was hailed as "the poet of Christmas." Just as [[Thomas_Kingo]] was known as "the poet of Easter," Brorson became the voice of the Nativity in Danish hymnody.

In 1747 he was appointed by King Christian VI to become bishop of the diocese of Ribe. The story is told that when the king met Brorson and learned he was the author of "Awake, all things that God has made," he promised him the bishopric on the spot.

## Sorrow and Swan Song

The later years of Brorson's life were darkened by grief. In the very year he was elevated to the bishopric, his beloved wife died while giving birth to their thirteenth child. Other sorrows followed. His thoughts turned more and more toward heaven, and a celestial radiance is reflected in the hymns of his *Swan-Song* collection. This is especially true of "Behold a host, arrayed in white":

> Behold a host, arrayed in white,
> Like thousand snow-clad mountains bright,
> With palms they stand --- who are this band
>   Before the throne of light?
> Lo, these are they, of glorious fame,
> Who from the great affliction came,
> And in the flood of Jesus' blood
>   Are cleansed from guilt and blame...

The hymn has become a favorite in America as well as in Europe through its association with Edvard Grieg's famous musical setting.

## The Symbols of Three Hymnists

The Danish hymnologist Brandt compared the three great Danish hymnists through their characteristic symbols: Kingo's was the sun, Brorson's the rose, and Grundtvig's the bird. Brorson glorifies Christ as the Friend of the spiritually poor and needy. His people learn to know Christ in the secret prayer chamber --- as the Rose that spreads its quiet fragrance.

Other well-known hymns by Brorson include:

- **"Thy little ones, dear Lord, are we"** --- a children's Christmas hymn
- **"O Father, may Thy Word prevail"**
- **"Jesus, Name of wondrous grace"**
- **"I see Thee standing, Lamb of God"**
- **"By faith we are divinely sure"**

## Legacy

Hans Adolph Brorson gave the Danish Church its warmest, most intimate voice. His hymns draw close to the Saviour with the tenderness of a child. He died in 1764, and his son gave the world his swan song the following year. It is one of the most beautiful last gifts in the history of Christian song.
