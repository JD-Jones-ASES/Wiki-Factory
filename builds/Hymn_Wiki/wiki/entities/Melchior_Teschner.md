# Melchior Teschner

Melchior Teschner was a Prussian musician who served as Precentor at Frauenstadt, Silesia, around 1613. Little is known of his life beyond this fact, but his musical legacy endures in every Palm Sunday procession in the Christian world.

## St. Theodulph

Teschner's harmony "St. Theodulph" is the standard musical setting for the great Palm Sunday processional "All Glory, Laud and Honor" — the Latin hymn written in the 9th century by [[Theodulph_of_Orleans]] while imprisoned in the cloister of Anjou. The tune appears in the *Methodist Hymnal* and in many other standard hymnals. Butterworth-Brown write that it "well represents the march of the bishop's Latin."

The hymn text was translated into English by [[John_Mason_Neale]], whose rendering — "All glory, laud, and honor / To Thee, Redeemer, King" — is the form universally sung in Protestant as well as Catholic churches. Teschner's tune carries that processional march with a stateliness perfectly suited to the ancient occasion it commemorates: the entry of Christ into Jerusalem.

## Musical and Thematic Context

Teschner represents the German Lutheran tradition of congregational psalmody in the early seventeenth century, when the Reformation's musical impulse was still reshaping the song-worship of European churches. Various modern composers had attempted settings of Theodulph's hymn, but Teschner's "St. Theodulph" proved the most enduring. Its dignity and forward motion have kept it in continuous use for four centuries.
