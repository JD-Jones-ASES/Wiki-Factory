# Horatio Richmond Palmer

Horatio Richmond Palmer combined the roles of composer, publisher, educator, and choral organizer in ways that made him one of the most influential figures in late-nineteenth-century American church music. His name is attached to one hymn above all others, but his influence ran far deeper than any single composition.

## Life

Palmer was born in Sherburne, New York, on April 26, 1834. He developed early as a musician and spent his career in New York, where he became director of the Church Choral Union --- a large-scale organization that brought choral singing into congregations across the city and region.

## "Yield Not to Temptation"

Palmer wrote "Yield Not to Temptation" in 1868. The text and tune together form one of the most direct moral exhortations in the gospel song tradition: a word-by-word encouragement to resist sin, addressed to a listener who is understood to be struggling. The tune is firm without being harsh, and the chorus contains what has become one of the best-known lines of the entire genre.

## The Song Queen

As a publisher, Palmer issued the collection *Song Queen*, which circulated in enormous numbers --- reportedly over 200,000 copies. This scale of distribution placed his editorial judgments and musical choices directly in the hands of Sunday-school teachers and choir leaders across the country, giving him influence that no single composition could have achieved.

## The Church Choral Union

Palmer's work with the Church Choral Union of New York represented a different kind of ambition: not individual hymns for individual hearts, but large-scale choral performance for urban congregations. His leadership there placed him in an unusual position straddling the world of gospel song and the world of classical sacred music.
