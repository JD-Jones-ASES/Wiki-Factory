# Isaac Baker Woodbury

Isaac Baker Woodbury belonged to the circle of Lowell Mason's pupils and contemporaries who shaped American sacred music in the mid-nineteenth century. He had a brief but productive career, cut short at thirty-nine by illness.

## Life

Woodbury was born in 1819 and showed early musical gifts. He studied in London and Paris, returning with a command of European musical techniques that set him apart from many of his American contemporaries. He became a compiler of music books and a teacher, working alongside Lowell Mason, William Bradbury, and George F. Root in the network of musical conventions and institutes that spread musical literacy across the country.

He died in 1858, having compressed into less than forty years a substantial body of work. John S. Dwight, the influential Boston music critic, attacked the methods of Mason, Woodbury, and others for "degrading and cheapening music" --- a charge that L.O. Emerson later indignantly rebutted, arguing that their work of spreading choral music to thousands of "musically hungry singers" was precisely what the country needed. (Metcalf, p. 214-215)

## Music

Woodbury compiled multiple books of sacred music and composed hymn tunes that appeared in many mid-century collections. His work was part of the broad movement to provide American churches with accessible, singable, and devotionally sound music. Like several of his contemporaries, his tunes were more popular in his own generation than they have proven in the long run --- but the conventions and institutes he participated in helped create the infrastructure for American congregational singing that persists to this day.
