## Text

> *From [[The_Christian_Hymn_Book|The Christian Hymn Book]] (1870), Hymn 282*

```
Why will ye waste on trifling cares
  That life which God’s compassion spares;
  While, in the various range of thought,
  The one thing needful is forgot?

  2 Shall God invite you from above?
  Shall Jesus urge his dying love?
  Shall troubled conscience give you pain?
  And all these pleas unite in vain?

  3 Not so your eyes will always view
  Those objects which you now pursue:
  Not so will heaven and hell appear
  When death’s decisive hour is near.

  4 Almighty God! thy power impart;
  Fix deep conviction on each heart;
  Nor let us waste on trifling cares
  That life which thy compassion spares.
```

## Historical Context

### From Duffield (1886)

It may be worth our while to quote it here in the original form as a sufficient reply to those who so often object to any alteration of the language of a hymn : ' Why will ye lavish out your years Amidst a thousand trifling cares ? Doddridge's Hymn 206, " The Care of the Soul the one Thing Needful. — Luke 10:42." It is also a remarkable example of the improvement effected in a grand hymn by a little modification. Every stanza of the five shows the effects of judicious amendment.
