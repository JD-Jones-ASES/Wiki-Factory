## Text

> *From [[The_Christian_Hymn_Book|The Christian Hymn Book]] (1870), Hymn 1195*

```
A sweetly solemn thought,
    Comes to me o’er and o’er;
  To-day, I’m nearer to my home
    Than e’er I’ve been before.

  2 Nearer my Father’s house,
    Where many mansions be,
  And nearer to the great white throne,
    Nearer the crystal sea;

  3 Nearer the bound of life,
    Where falls my burden down;
  Nearer to where I leave my cross,
    And where I gain my crown.

  4 Saviour, confirm my trust,
    Complete my faith in thee;
  And let me feel as if I stood
    Close on eternity;

  5 Feel as if now my feet
    Were slipping o’er the brink;
  For I may now be nearer home,
    Much nearer than I think.
```

## Historical Context

### From Duffield (1886)

At the age of seventeen she wrote this hymn. It is a matter of thankfulness that we have from her own pen the information we seek as to the origin of this hymn, and also that she was aware of the storj' which we append and which greatly cheered her. She wrote in the last year of her life : " I inclose the hymn and the story for you, not because I am vain of the notice, but because I thought you would feel a peculiar interest in them when you knew the hymn was written eighteen years ago (1852), in your house.
