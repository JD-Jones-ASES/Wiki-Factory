## Text

> *From [[The_Christian_Hymn_Book|The Christian Hymn Book]] (1870), Hymn 1311*

```
Thou Grace divine, encircling all,
    A soundless, shoreless sea!
  Wherein at last, our souls shall fall,
    O Love of God most free!

  2 When over dizzy steeps we go,
    One soft hand blinds our eyes,
  The other leads us safe and slow,
    O Love of God most wise!

  3 And though we turn us from thy face,
    And wander wide and long,
  Thou holdest us still in thine embrace,
    O Love of God most strong!

  4 The saddened heart, the restless soul,
    The toilworn frame and mind,
  Alike confess thy sweet control,
    O Love of God most kind!

  5 But not alone thy care we claim,
    Our wayward steps to win:
  We know thee by a dearer name,
    O Love of God within!

  6 And filled and quickened by thy breath,
    Our souls are strong and free
  To rise o’er sin, and fear, and death,
    O Love of God, to thee!
```

## Historical Context

### From Duffield (1886)

That the author of two of our finest Christmas hymns should be the maternal uncle of one of our sweetest singers is not an unfore- seen event. The present writer, mousing one day among the rubbish of an old book-Stall, happened upon a small, neat wlume, entitled Ilymms and Sonnets, by E S. Her various poems and hymns have been handed about in manuscript, and this present one had the good fortune to meet the eye of her uncle, Rev.

And the hymn which heads this notice was so admirable in every way that the question at once arose : " To what extent is this writer known ?" It was not hard to connect the verses with the name of Miss Eliza Scudder, nor to ascertain, when once her hymns were differ- entiated from the mass, that she had obtained four places in Hymns of the Spirit.
