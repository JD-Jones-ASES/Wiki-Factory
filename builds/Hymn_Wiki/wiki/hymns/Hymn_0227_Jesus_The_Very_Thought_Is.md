## Text

> *From [[The_Christian_Hymn_Book|The Christian Hymn Book]] (1870), Hymn 227*

```
Jesus! the very thought is sweet;
  In that dear name all heart-joys meet;
  But sweeter than the honey far
  The glimpses of his presence are.

  2 No word is sung more sweet than this;
  No name is heard more full of bliss;
  No thought brings sweeter comfort nigh,
  Than Jesus, Son of God, most high.

  3 Jesus, the hope of souls forlorn!
  How good to them for sin that mourn;
  To them that seek thee, O how kind!
  But what art thou to them that find?

  4 No tongue of mortal can express,
  No letters write its blessedness;
  Alone, who hath thee in his heart,
  Knows, love of Jesus, what thou art.
```

## Historical Context

### From Duffield (1886)

Edward Caswall' s almost unapproachably fine translation of St. Bernard of Clairvaulx's hymn, '' Jesu, dulcis memoria,'' of which the full account can be found in " The Latin Hymn-Writers and their Hymns." Caswall's rendering is in fifty stanzas, and first appeared in his Lyra Catholica, 1849. It is in this hymn that the great theologian and scholar, and preacher of crusades, has set the key for modern hymnody.

Caswall's hymn has comforted at least one soul in the dying hour : Rev.
