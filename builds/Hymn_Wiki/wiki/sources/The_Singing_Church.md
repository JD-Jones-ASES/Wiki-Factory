## Overview

Edmund S. Lorenz's *The Singing Church* is a comprehensive pastoral guide to Christian hymnody. Unlike purely academic treatments, Lorenz writes for church leaders who need both historical understanding and practical wisdom for using hymns in worship. The book traces the Christian hymn from its biblical origins through the 20th century, combining theological analysis with guidance on hymn selection, study, and congregational practice.

## Structure

The book is organized into three parts with 22 chapters:

### Part I: The Character of the Hymn (Chapters I–VI)
1. **What Is a Hymn?** — Definitions, essential qualities, types
2. **The Purpose and Value of Hymns** — Worship, teaching, comfort, spiritual atmosphere
3. **The Literary Aspect of Hymns** — Balance between poetic quality and spiritual content
4. **The Emendation of Hymns** — How hymns are edited across centuries; principles of fair revision
5. **The Content of the Hymn** — Classification of hymn subjects: theological, doctrinal, meditative
6. **The Gospel Hymn** — Defense of the Gospel hymn's practical effectiveness

### Part II: History of the Development of the Christian Hymn (Chapters VII–XVIII)
7. **Apostolic and Post-Apostolic Hymns** — NT canticles, early church singing
8. **The Greek Church** — Gregory of Nazianzus, Synesius, John of Damascus
9. **The Latin Hymn** — Ambrose, medieval sequences
10. **Late Medieval Hymnody** — Scholastic and devotional traditions
11. **Martin Luther and His Period** — "Ein' feste Burg," vernacular singing
12. **German Hymnody After Luther** — Pietism, Gerhardt, Tersteegen
13. **Metrical Psalmody** — Marot, Sternhold & Hopkins, Tate & Brady
14. **The English Hymn Before Watts** — How literary ideals delayed hymn development
15. **Isaac Watts and His Period** — Free hymnody, "renovation of psalmody"
16. **The Wesleys and Their Era** — John as editor, Charles as prolific hymnist; Newton, Cowper, Toplady
17. **Hymns in the Church of England** — Heber, Keble, Milman, Oxford Movement
18. **American Hymnody** — From psalmody through new American voices

### Part III: Practical Hymnology (Chapters XIX–XXII)
19. **The Study of Hymns** — Methods for systematic hymn study
20. **The Practical Use of Hymns** — Integration into worship services
21. **The Selection of Hymns** — Criteria for choosing appropriate hymns
22. **The Announcement and Treatment of Hymns** — Presenting hymns to congregations

## Distinctive Contributions

### Watts vs. Wesley Framework
Lorenz articulates a defining contrast: Watts represents **objective majesty** (God's glory, doctrinal truth), while Wesley represents **subjective experience** (personal encounter, emotional response). Both are essential; together they define the full range of congregational hymnody.

### Hymn Emendation Analysis
Chapter IV documents the editorial history of major hymns with specific textual examples. Lorenz catalogs more than 30 documented variations of "Jesus, Lover of My Soul" across different hymnals, and shows how "Rock of Ages" had only 11 of 24 lines unchanged in some editions.

### Defense of the Gospel Hymn
Against literary critics who dismissed Gospel hymns as artistically inferior, Lorenz argues for their practical efficacy in evangelism and personal devotion, drawing a distinction between the hymnal tradition and the Gospel song tradition.

### Practical Pastoral Guidance
Part III is unique among hymnological works in providing concrete guidance for pastors on studying, selecting, and presenting hymns in worship — material not found in academic treatments.

## Scripture Usage

Lorenz references Scripture extensively (138+ documented citations), grounding his discussion in biblical authority. Key passages include the Ephesians 5:18-19 epigraph, Old Testament canticles (Exodus 15, Psalms), New Testament hymnic passages (Colossians 3:16, Revelation doxologies), and Paul's singing in prison (Acts 16:25).
