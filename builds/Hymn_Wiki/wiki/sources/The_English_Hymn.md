## Overview

Louis F. Benson's *The English Hymn* is a magisterial study of how hymn-singing developed and spread across the English-speaking Christian world. Unlike biographical works that organize by author, Benson follows the institutional path — which denominations adopted hymns, when, under what theological controversy, and with what resulting hymn books.

## Structure

The work is organized into 11 chapters tracing the major movements that shaped English-language hymnody:

1. **The Evolution of the English Hymn** — from metrical psalms through devotional poetry
2. **The Liturgical Use of English Hymns** — Playford, Baxter, Keach, and the denominational divisions at the Restoration
3–4. **Doctor Watts' "Renovation of Psalmody"** — Watts' system of praise and its dominance in England and America
5–6. **The Hymnody of the Methodist Revival** — the Wesleys, Moravian hymnody, and American Methodism
7. **The Hymnody of the Evangelical Revival** — Whitefield, Lady Huntingdon, the Olney Hymns
8. **The Evangelical Hymnody in America** — Baptists, Congregationalists, Presbyterians, Episcopalians
9. **The Hymnody of the Romantic Movement** — the literary hymn, Heber, and the Gospel Hymn
10. **The Hymnody of the Oxford Revival** — Tractarian hymnals, *Hymns Ancient and Modern*, Neale
11. **Twentieth Century Hymnody** — new theology, social democracy, and modern standards

## Distinctive Contribution

Benson covers denominational traditions that other sources in this wiki treat lightly or not at all: [[Moravian_Hymnody|Moravian]], Universalist, Swedenborgian, Shaker, Adventist, and even Mormon hymnody. His treatment of the "Psalmody Controversy" in American Presbyterianism and the gradual adoption of hymns across resistant denominations is unique among our sources. The book also provides the most detailed account of the camp meeting hymn tradition and the rise of the Gospel Hymn.
