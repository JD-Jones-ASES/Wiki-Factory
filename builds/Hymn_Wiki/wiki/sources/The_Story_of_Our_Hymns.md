# The Story of Our Hymns

By [[Ernest_Edwin_Ryden|Ernest Edwin Ryden]], Pastor of Gloria Dei Lutheran Church, St. Paul, Minnesota. Published by Augustana Book Concern, Rock Island, Illinois, 1930.

## Overview

Ryden's work is the **biographical and chronological backbone** of the Hymn Wiki. It traces the history of Christian hymnody from the earliest church chants through the early 20th century, organized into five major sections by national and cultural tradition.

The book was inspired by Ryden's desire to create deeper love for the great lyrics of the Christian Church. He adopted a popular style to appeal to lay readers, presenting the historical and personal circumstances that gave birth to beloved hymns.

## Structure

### Part I: Early Christian Hymnody
Covers the first centuries of Christian worship music: the early chants, Greek and Syriac hymns, the rise of Latin hymnody, and the golden age of Latin hymn writing. Key figures include [[Ambrose_of_Milan|Ambrose]], [[Ephrem_the_Syrian|Ephrem Syrus]], and early church fathers.

### Part II: German Hymnody
The most extensive section. Covers [[Martin_Luther|Martin Luther]] as "Father of Evangelical Hymnody," the Reformation hymn writers, the Thirty Years' War period, [[Paul_Gerhardt]] as "Prince of Lutheran Hymnists," the Pietist school, Moravian hymnody, and the Age of Rationalism.

### Part III: Scandinavian Hymnody
Unique coverage not found in other sources. Swedish, Danish, and Norwegian hymn traditions including [[Thomas_Kingo|Kingo]] (Poet of Easter-Tide), [[Hans_Adolph_Brorson|Brorson]] (Poet of Christmas), and [[N_F_S_Grundtvig|Grundtvig]] (Poet of Whitsuntide).

### Part IV: English Hymnody
From the dawn of English hymnody through [[Isaac_Watts]], [[Philip_Doddridge]], the [[Charles_Wesley|Wesley brothers]], [[John_Newton]], [[Reginald_Heber]], [[Frances_Ridley_Havergal]], and many others. Includes notable chapters on specific famous hymns and their stories.

### Part V: American Hymnody
The development of hymn writing in America from the colonial period through [[Fanny_Crosby]], covering patriotic hymnists, women hymn writers, and the gospel song movement.

## Unique Contributions

- **Only source covering early Christian and Syriac hymnody** in detail
- **Most thorough Scandinavian hymnody coverage** of any English-language popular work
- **Deepest biographical narratives** (2,000-5,000 words per major figure)
- **Chronological structure** revealing how hymnody evolved through historical and spiritual movements
- **Translation history** documenting how ancient hymns reached English congregations

## Perspective and Limitations

Written from a Lutheran perspective, which gives special depth to German and Scandinavian coverage but may underrepresent other traditions. Published in 1930, so coverage ends before the mid-20th century. Ryden occasionally reflects the scholarly conventions of his era.
