# The Story of the Hymns and Tunes

By [[Theron_Brown|Theron Brown]] and [[Hezekiah_Butterworth|Hezekiah Butterworth]]. Published in New York, 1906.

## Overview

This work combines the musical and textual history of Christian hymns into a single narrative, organized thematically rather than chronologically. It provides **unique coverage of tune history and musical composition** not found in the other sources.

The book originated as two separate earlier works by Hezekiah Butterworth: *The Story of the Hymns* and *The Story of the Tunes*. When Butterworth died before completing a revision, he entrusted the manuscript to Theron Brown, who combined, revised, and completed the work.

## Structure

The book contains 14 thematic chapters:

1. **Hymns of Praise and Worship** --- foundational hymns of Christian adoration
2. **Some Hymns of Great Witnesses** --- hymns connected to notable Christian testimony
3. **Hymns of Christian Devotion and Experience** --- personal faith and spiritual growth
4. **Missionary Hymns** --- songs of evangelism and global mission
5. **Hymns of Suffering and Trust** --- hymns born from trials and perseverance
6. **Christian Ballads** --- narrative hymns telling stories of faith
7. **Old Revival Hymns** --- songs of awakening and camp meetings
8. **Sunday School Hymns** --- hymns for Christian education
9. **Patriotic Hymns** --- national and civic hymns
10. **Sailor's Hymns** --- maritime hymns
11. **Hymns of Wales** --- the Welsh hymn tradition
12. **Field Hymns** --- hymns of labor, nature, and the outdoors
13. **Hymns, Festival and Occasional** --- seasonal and special occasion hymns
14. **Hymns of Hope and Consolation** --- comfort in death and the promise of heaven

## Unique Contributions

- **Musical history depth:** Covers tune names, composers, and the development of congregational music from Gregorian chant through 19th-century gospel songs
- **Thematic organization:** Groups hymns by function and feeling rather than chronology, offering a different lens than Ryden
- **Welsh hymnody:** Dedicated chapter on the Welsh tradition not found in other sources
- **Anecdotal richness:** Vivid stories of how hymns were composed, discovered, or transformed by specific circumstances
- **26 biographical portraits:** Photographic reproductions of major figures

## Relationship to Other Sources

Overlaps significantly with [[The_Story_of_Our_Hymns|Ryden]] in covering major figures like [[Isaac_Watts]], [[Charles_Wesley]], [[Martin_Luther]], and [[Reginald_Heber]], but provides different details, especially about musical settings and tune history. Where Ryden goes deeper on biography, Butterworth-Brown goes deeper on musical context and anecdote.
