## Overview

*A Book of Hymns for Public and Private Devotion* is a significant mid-19th-century American hymnal compiled by two Unitarian ministers, Samuel Longfellow and Samuel Johnson. First published in 1848, it reached its fifteenth edition by 1866, demonstrating broad and sustained use in liberal Protestant worship. The collection of 600 hymns draws from the full breadth of English hymnody while including distinctive Unitarian compositions and social-justice themes uncommon in more orthodox hymnals of the period.

## Structure

The hymnal is organized into 8 major thematic sections with a supplement integrated into the main body:

1. **Hymns of Worship** (Hymns 1–70, 480–503) — Opening, prayers, praise, closing
2. **God** (Hymns 71–101, 247–267) — Being, love, providence, communion in prayer
3. **Jesus Christ** (Hymns 102–138) — Advent, mission, life, suffering, resurrection
4. **Communion Hymns** (Hymns 139–164, 560–564) — Remembrance, cross, invitations
5. **Christianity and the Kingdom of Heaven** (Hymns 165–192, 584–600) — Gospel, church, brotherhood, peace, liberty
6. **The Inward Character** (Hymns 193–289, 510–538) — Penitence, aspiration, trust, faith, charity
7. **The Outward Life** (Hymns 290–361, 539–549) — Action, conflict, affliction, death, spiritual world
8. **Various Occasions** (Hymns 362–479, 550–582) — Morning, evening, baptism, marriage, ordination, funeral, missions, seasons, patriotic, slavery, temperance

## Distinctive Contributions

### Social Themes
Unlike most hymnals of the period, this collection includes sections on **American Slavery** (Hymns 436–440, 581–582), **Temperance** (576–577), **Peace** (419–422), and **Reform** (191, 426–429, 592), reflecting the Unitarian social conscience of the era.

### Editorial Practice
An asterisk (*) before an author's name indicates modifications made by the compilers — a practice documented in the preface that transparently marks emendations.

### Hymn Format
Each hymn includes: number, meter abbreviation, author attribution, thematic title, and numbered stanzas. No scripture references are provided in the body text.

## Overlap with The Christian Hymn Book

Many hymns from major authors (Watts, Wesley, Doddridge, Cowper, Montgomery, Heber, Newton) appear in both this collection and [[The_Christian_Hymn_Book]], sometimes with textual variations reflecting different editorial traditions. See individual hymn pages for cross-references.
