# American Writers and Compilers of Sacred Music

## Overview

Metcalf's work is the standard biographical dictionary of American sacred music composers. Organized strictly by birth year, each entry follows a consistent template: biographical narrative, list of published compilations, and inventory of surviving tunes with their hymn-text pairings.

## Structure

- **Part I** (1689–1757): John Tufts through Daniel Read — the early New England singing masters
- **Part II** (1758–1772): Timothy Swan through Stephen Jenks — the second generation
- **Part III** (1773–1784): Abraham Maxim through Thomas Hastings — expansion beyond New England
- **Part IV** (1785–1800): Samuel Dyer through Samuel A. Ward — Lowell Mason, Bradbury, Root
- **Part V** (topical): Revivalist Group, Camp Meeting Music, Washington Hymnody

## Research Method

Metcalf's methodology was systematic. For each major composer, he surveyed a fixed set of modern hymnals (10-30 books depending on the chapter) to count how many still used each tune. He corresponded directly with descendants and colleagues, consulted church records, newspaper archives (including death notices), library manuscript collections, and copyright records. The book corrects many errors in previous biographical dictionaries. Many chapters originated as articles in *The Choir Herald* magazine, which Metcalf edited.

Key primary sources used: Family Bibles (e.g., the Billings family Bible for birth/death dates); personal correspondence with composers' descendants (e.g., Webb's daughter); copyright records at the Library of Congress; *The Musical Cyclopedia* of John W. Moore; George Hood's *History of Music in New England* (1846).

## Distinctive Contribution

This is the only source in the wiki collection that focuses specifically on **tune composers**. Where Ryden and Butterworth-Brown mention tunes incidentally, Metcalf provides composer biographies, publication histories, and crucially, **which tunes pair with which hymn texts**. This data directly fills the empty `composer` and `tune_name` fields on our 1,324 hymn pages. Key figures include [[William_Billings]], [[Oliver_Holden]], [[Lowell_Mason]], [[Thomas_Hastings]], [[William_Bradbury]], [[George_Frederick_Root]], and many lesser-known early American composers.

Metcalf establishes that three men — [[Lowell_Mason]], [[Thomas_Hastings]], and [[William_Bradbury]] — "led the prevailing school of sacred music at the beginning of the last century."
