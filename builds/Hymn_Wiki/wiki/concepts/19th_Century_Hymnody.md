# 19th Century Hymnody

> [[_overview|Home]] > [[_index|Index]] > Concepts

The nineteenth century is the golden age of English and American hymnody. No other period in Christian history produced such a quantity of excellent sacred song across so many traditions, so many nations, and so many theological emphases. From the intimate personal lyric to the great missionary anthem, from the Oxford Movement's recovery of ancient treasures to the camp-meeting gospel song, the nineteenth century has given the church more of its living repertoire than any other era.

## The English Lyricists

The century opens with [[Reginald_Heber]] (1783-1826), who brought genuine poetic craftsmanship to English hymnody. Heber was a Church of England rector who felt acutely that his church had nothing to compare with the Watts and Wesley treasury of Dissent and Methodism. He began writing hymns for his congregation, aiming for quality that could stand alongside the greatest English poetry. He was eventually appointed Bishop of Calcutta and died young on the mission field.

[[Henry_Francis_Lyte]] (1793-1847), another Anglican, wrote with a tenderness born of chronic illness and approaching death. His most famous hymn was composed on the last Sunday he was well enough to take a service; shortly afterward he sailed for the Mediterranean to die.

[[George_Matheson]] (1842-1906) wrote what he himself called his "swift composition" --- a hymn composed in five minutes on a dark personal evening, blind and in pain --- that has become one of the most beloved in the tradition.

## The Oxford Movement and Ancient Recovery

The Oxford Movement of the 1830s-1840s transformed Anglican worship and, with it, Anglican hymnody. Figures like [[John_Keble]] (whose *Christian Year* had already become a devotional classic) and [[John_Henry_Newman]] brought a new seriousness to liturgy, music, and the church's historical heritage.

The great scholarly achievement of the movement for hymnody was the work of [[John_Mason_Neale]] (1818-1866), who translated an enormous treasury of Greek and Latin hymns into singable English verse. Through Neale, Victorian congregations gained access to [[Medieval_Hymnody]] and [[Early_Christian_Hymnody]] that had been inaccessible for centuries.

*Hymns Ancient and Modern* (1861), the great Anglican hymnal that synthesized ancient and modern in a single collection, shaped Anglican worship for generations. See [[Anglican_Hymnody]] for the full story of this tradition.

## Women Hymn-Writers

The nineteenth century is also the great age of women in hymnody. See [[Women_in_Hymnody]] for the full account, but the roll call alone is impressive: [[Charlotte_Elliott]] (1789-1871), [[Sarah_Flower_Adams]] (1805-1848), [[Frances_Ridley_Havergal]] (1836-1879), [[Fanny_Crosby]] (1820-1915), [[Kate_Hankey]], [[Anna_Warner]], [[Annie_Sherwood_Hawks]], and many more.

[[Fanny_Crosby]], though largely a figure of the latter nineteenth century and beyond, is the greatest single hymn-writer America has produced. Blind from infancy, she wrote over eight thousand hymns --- a creative output that staggers the imagination. Her gospel songs shaped American evangelical worship in ways that are still being felt.

## American Hymnody Comes of Age

The United States developed its own distinctive hymnody during this century. The shaping influences included [[Lowell_Mason]] and [[Thomas_Hastings]], who brought European musical standards to bear on American congregational singing. The Sunday school movement generated an enormous demand for simple, tuneful songs for children. The great [[Revival_Hymns|revival]] tradition, culminating in the Moody-Sankey campaigns of the 1870s, produced the [[Gospel_Songs|gospel song]] --- a new popular form distinct from the classical hymn.

The [[Missionary_Hymns|missionary hymn movement]] gave the century some of its most stirring songs. Heber's missionary anthem is the most famous example, but the genre extended across denominations.

## Scottish and Scots-Irish Voices

[[Horatius_Bonar]] (1808-1889) gave Scottish Presbyterianism its warmest singing voice. His hymns of personal faith and assurance brought something of the warmth associated with Methodism into the more reserved Presbyterian tradition. [[Anne_Ross_Cousin]] and [[Elizabeth_Clephane]] also contributed beautiful hymns from the Scottish stream.

## Legacy

The nineteenth century's legacy is so vast that it is almost impossible to summarize. Pick up any traditional Protestant hymnal and count the hymns from 1800-1900: they will constitute the majority. The century had the double blessing of a Romantic emphasis on feeling and individual expression and an evangelical awakening that gave that feeling a theological home.
