# 20th Century Hymnody

> [[_overview|Home]] > [[_index|Index]] > Concepts

This wiki's primary sources --- *The Story of Our Hymns* and *The Story of the Hymns and Tunes* --- were written in the early decades of the twentieth century, and their coverage naturally thins as it approaches the authors' own time. What they give us for the twentieth century is less a survey than a glimpse: the early decades of a new era seen from close range, without the perspective that only time provides.

## What Our Sources Cover

The twentieth century that appears in these pages is primarily the legacy of the nineteenth. The great [[Gospel_Songs|gospel song]] tradition continued into the new century through figures whose careers had begun in the Moody-Sankey era. [[Fanny_Crosby]], whose hymn-writing spanned from the 1860s all the way to her death in 1915, bridged the centuries in her own person.

The [[Sunday_School_Hymns|Sunday school movement]] remained a powerful force in shaping congregational singing, particularly in America. The camp meeting and revival traditions continued, and the hymnal --- the bound collection of standard congregational song --- was still the primary vehicle for hymnody in most churches.

## The Limits of This Wiki

This wiki does not attempt comprehensive coverage of twentieth-century hymnody beyond what our sources explicitly discuss. The rich developments that followed --- the liturgical renewal movements, the folk hymn explosion of the 1960s, the rise of contemporary Christian music, the global hymnody explosion of the late twentieth century --- lie beyond the scope of our source documents.

Readers who wish to follow the story beyond 1930 will find it in other places. What this wiki offers is deep, detailed coverage of the long tradition that made the twentieth century's developments possible: the patristic age, the medieval monasteries, the Reformation, the German chorale, the Wesleys, Watts, the Victorian golden age, and the American gospel song. That is the foundation on which all subsequent hymnody rests.

## A Note on Living Tradition

Hymnody has never been a finished product. Every era the church has passed through, it has sung --- and in singing, it has added to the treasury. The hymns covered in this wiki are not museum pieces. They are living songs, still heard every Sunday in churches around the world. The tradition continues.
