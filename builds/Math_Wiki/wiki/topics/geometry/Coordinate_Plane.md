# Coordinate Plane

> Stub — expanded in Phase 2 with content from the ingested source books.

The **coordinate plane** is the foundation for all analytic geometry. Every point in the plane has two numbers as its address: an $x$-coordinate (how far right or left) and a $y$-coordinate (how far up or down). The point $(0, 0)$ is called the **origin**, and it sits where the two axes cross.

Once points have addresses, every shape you care about — lines, circles, parabolas, triangles — becomes an equation in $x$ and $y$. That's what "analytic" geometry means: geometry you can do with algebra.

## Quadrants

The two axes split the plane into four **quadrants**:

- Quadrant I: $x > 0$, $y > 0$ (upper right)
- Quadrant II: $x < 0$, $y > 0$ (upper left)
- Quadrant III: $x < 0$, $y < 0$ (lower left)
- Quadrant IV: $x > 0$, $y < 0$ (lower right)
