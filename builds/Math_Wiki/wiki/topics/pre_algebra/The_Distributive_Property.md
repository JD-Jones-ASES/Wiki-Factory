# The Distributive Property

The **distributive property** tells you how multiplication interacts with a sum or a difference held inside parentheses. Instead of adding first and then multiplying, you may multiply the outside factor by each inside term separately and then combine the pieces. The two ways give the same answer, but one is sometimes much easier than the other.

In symbols:

$$
a(b + c) = ab + ac
$$

The subtraction version works exactly the same way:

$$
a(b - c) = ab - ac
$$

The outside factor can also sit on the right side without changing anything: $(b + c)a = ba + ca$. Multiplication is flexible about which side the factor lives on.

---

## Why it works: the area picture

Picture a rectangle whose height is $a$ and whose base is split into two pieces of length $b$ and $c$. The total base measures $b + c$, so the whole rectangle covers $a(b + c)$ square units. But you can also count the two smaller rectangles on the left and the right: one has area $ab$, the other has area $ac$, and together they cover $ab + ac$ square units. The same shape, two ways of measuring — which is exactly what the property says.

This geometric picture is why the distributive property shows up constantly in area word problems, in mental arithmetic, and later in every chapter of algebra.

---

## Example 1: a plain numerical check

> Use the property to evaluate $4(6 + 5)$, then check the answer by adding first.

Hand the factor $4$ to each term inside the parentheses:

$$
4(6 + 5) = 4 \cdot 6 + 4 \cdot 5
$$

Multiply each piece:

$$
= 24 + 20
$$

Add:

$$
= 44
$$

Now verify by the order of operations — add inside the parentheses first, then multiply:

$$
4(6 + 5) = 4 \cdot 11 = 44
$$

Both roads end at the same place.

---

## Example 2: a negative factor with subtraction

> Evaluate $-2(9 - 4)$.

Keep the sign of the factor glued to it while you distribute. The factor is $-2$:

$$
-2(9 - 4) = (-2)(9) - (-2)(4)
$$

Multiply each product:

$$
= -18 - (-8)
$$

Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive, so:

$$
= -18 + 8 = -10
$$

Check by simplifying the parentheses first: $9 - 4 = 5$, and $-2 \cdot 5 = -10$. Signs match.

When the factor out front is negative, every term inside picks up a sign flip. Missing that is one of the most common mistakes in pre-algebra — slow down and watch both signs.

---

## Example 3 (optional): mental-math shortcut

The distributive property is not only a rule for homework — it is the trick your brain actually uses for clean mental arithmetic. Suppose someone asks you to find $6 \times 104$ without a calculator. Break $104$ into friendly pieces:

$$
6 \times 104 = 6(100 + 4) = 600 + 24 = 624
$$

Or go the other way with subtraction for a number just under a round figure: $8 \times 99 = 8(100 - 1) = 800 - 8 = 792$. Both moves rely on the same property.

---

## Bridge to algebra

So far every example has used plain numbers inside the parentheses. The real power of the distributive property shows up when one of the terms inside is a letter. The rule is unchanged — you still hand the outside factor to each inside term — but now the results look like algebraic expressions:

$$
5(x + 3) = 5x + 15
$$

That step is the whole subject of [[The_Distributive_Property_With_Variables]], where you'll also use the property in reverse (pulling a common factor back out of a sum). Pairing that skill with [[Simplifying_Expressions]] and [[Multi_Step_Equations]] is how you start moving from arithmetic into real algebra.

---

## Common pitfalls

- **Division does not distribute across a sum the way multiplication does.** You cannot split $\dfrac{12}{4 + 2}$ into $\dfrac{12}{4} + \dfrac{12}{2}$. Always evaluate the denominator first.
- **Forgetting one of the terms.** When you distribute, every term inside the parentheses must be touched by the outside factor. Missing the second or third term is the number-one slip.
- **Losing a sign.** A negative outside factor flips the sign of every inside term; a minus inside the parentheses keeps its minus even after you distribute.
