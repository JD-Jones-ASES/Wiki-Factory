# Fractions, Decimals, and Percents

If you ever asked for "half" of a pizza, noted that the tank was at "point five," or read "fifty percent off" on a sale tag, you already worked with three different names for the same amount. Fractions, decimals, and percents are three languages that describe the same thing: a part of a whole. One sounds like ratios ($\tfrac{1}{2}$), one sounds like place value ($0.5$), and one sounds like store signs ($50\%$). Learning to jump between them quickly is one of the most useful survival skills in pre-algebra, because different problems hand you the information in different flavors, and you get to choose the flavor that is easiest to work with.

## What it means

All three forms represent the same kind of quantity: a piece of a whole, or equivalently a ratio of part to whole. The conversions below are the bridges between them.

$$
\text{fraction} \longleftrightarrow \text{decimal} \longleftrightarrow \text{percent}
$$

A **fraction** like $\tfrac{3}{4}$ says "three parts out of four equal parts." A **decimal** like $0.75$ uses place value — seven tenths plus five hundredths — to mean the same amount. A **percent** like $75\%$ is hundredths written in a tidy shorthand: seventy-five per hundred. All three names point to the same slice.

## How it works

There are six direction pairs to memorize, but they boil down to three rules.

**Fraction to decimal.** Treat the fraction bar as division and divide the top by the bottom. You can do this by long division, a calculator, or — if the denominator is already $10$, $100$, or $1000$ — just by reading the place values off the numerator.

$$
\frac{3}{4} = 3 \div 4 = 0.75
$$

**Decimal to percent.** Decimals and percents differ by a factor of one hundred. To go from decimal to percent, multiply by one hundred and attach the percent sign.

$$
0.75 \cdot 100 = 75\%
$$

**Percent to decimal.** Reverse the previous move. Divide by one hundred and drop the percent sign.

$$
75\% = 75 \div 100 = 0.75
$$

The other direction pairs (fraction to percent and percent to fraction) are just two of the moves above stacked together. To go from a fraction to a percent, first turn it into a decimal, then turn the decimal into a percent. To go from a percent to a fraction, write the percent as hundredths and simplify.

$$
45\% = \frac{45}{100} = \frac{9}{20}
$$

## Why it works

The word **percent** literally means "per hundred" — _cent_ is the Latin root for a hundred (think century, cent, centimeter). So when you write $35\%$, you are writing a shorthand for "$35$ per hundred," which in fraction form is $\tfrac{35}{100}$ and in decimal form is $0.35$. All three names are legal ways to write the same ratio. A fraction makes the part-to-whole relationship obvious. A decimal makes place value obvious. A percent standardizes the whole at $100$, which is why percents are so handy for comparisons — you are always comparing to the same benchmark.

Multiplying or dividing by $100$ is just moving between "out of one" (decimal) and "out of one hundred" (percent). That is why decimal-to-percent conversion always shifts the decimal point two places. Two places is what a factor of $100$ looks like.

## Worked examples

### Example 1

Express $\tfrac{3}{8}$ as a decimal and then as a percent.

First, rewrite the fraction as division:

$$
\frac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8.
$$

Dividing $3$ by $8$ gives $0.375$. Now turn the decimal into a percent by multiplying by one hundred:

$$
0.375 \cdot 100 = 37.5\%.
$$

So $\tfrac{3}{8} = 0.375 = 37.5\%$. All three names refer to the same slice — a little over a third of the whole.

### Example 2

Compute a fraction and a percent equivalent to $0.25$.

The decimal $0.25$ is already saying "twenty-five hundredths," so writing it as a fraction is direct:

$$
0.25 = \frac{25}{100}.
$$

Simplify by dividing top and bottom by their greatest common factor, which is $25$:

$$
\frac{25}{100} = \frac{1}{4}.
$$

For the percent, multiply the decimal by one hundred:

$$
0.25 \cdot 100 = 25\%.
$$

Final answer: $0.25 = \tfrac{1}{4} = 25\%$. A quarter of the pie, however you prefer to label it.

### Example 3

Determine the fraction (in lowest terms) and the decimal that match $65\%$.

Start with the fraction. Percent means hundredths, so plant $65$ over $100$:

$$
65\% = \frac{65}{100}.
$$

Both numerator and denominator share a factor of $5$. Dividing top and bottom by $5$:

$$
\frac{65}{100} = \frac{13}{20}.
$$

For the decimal, divide by one hundred (or shift the decimal point two places to the left in your head):

$$
65\% = 0.65.
$$

Final answer: $65\% = \tfrac{13}{20} = 0.65$.

## Common pitfalls

- **The off-by-one-hundred error.** Writing $0.07 = 7\%$ instead of $0.07 = 7\%$ is fine; writing $0.7 = 7\%$ is not — it should be $70\%$. Double-check by multiplying by $100$.
- **Forgetting to simplify.** $\tfrac{50}{100}$ is a legal answer, but the clean, reduced form $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is what graders and later topics expect.
- **Confusing $0.5$ with $\tfrac{1}{5}$.** The decimal $0.5$ is five tenths, which equals $\tfrac{1}{2}$, not $\tfrac{1}{5}$. When in doubt, read the decimal out loud with its place value: "five tenths."
- **Treating mixed numbers as percents.** The mixed number $1\tfrac{1}{2}$ equals $150\%$, not $1.5\%$. Percents above $100$ are perfectly fine and mean "more than one whole."
